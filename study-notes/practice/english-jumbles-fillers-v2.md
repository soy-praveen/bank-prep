## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

### Part A — Para-Jumbles

**Set 1 — Order: B, C, A, D**
B opens with the full noun phrase and the time anchor "A decade ago" (the past scene). C contrasts with "Today." A introduces "this convenience came at a cost," pointing back to the tap-to-deliver ease in C. D names "The hidden price," resolving A.

- Q1: (2) B
- Q2: (1) A — "this convenience" in A refers to the ten-minute delivery described in C.
- Q3: (4) D

**Trick:** The opener must contain a full noun, not a pronoun or a back-reference. "A decade ago... local market" is a clean, self-standing statement; "this convenience," "the same," and "The hidden price" all lean on something earlier, so they cannot lead. Time markers *a decade ago -> today* fix the spine.

**Set 2 — Order: B, E, D, A, C**
B introduces "A teacher" (full noun) and the change. E's "the change" hugs B. D explains what each puzzle did. A gives the result "over the following weeks." C is the reflective conclusion ("what finally made the lessons stick").

- Q4: (2) B
- Q5: (5) DE is not consecutive; the correct consecutive pair among the options is... re-check: the order is B-E-D-A-C, so consecutive pairs are BE, ED, DA, AC. Option (2) AC is consecutive. Answer: (2) AC.
- Q6: (3) C

**Trick:** Pronouns hug their noun — "the change" in E must sit right after the sentence that introduces a change (B). The result word "began... for fun" (A) and the summary verb "finally made... stick" (C) mark the tail; a reflective, evaluative sentence usually closes a narrative.

**Set 3 — Order: B, C, A, D, E**
B states the dismissive view (full noun "Wetlands"). C flips it with "Yet." A explains "These reserves" (the storage system in C). D shows the consequence of destroying them. E ends with the ironic present-day fix ("now, belatedly").

- Q7: (2) B
- Q8: (2) C — A's "These reserves" refers to the "water-storage system" named in C.
- Q9: (5) E

**Trick:** *However / Yet* signals a reversal, so C cannot open — it needs a claim to overturn (B). "These reserves" is a back-pointer that must follow the noun it renames. The word "now, belatedly" marks the closing, up-to-the-present sentence.

**Set 4 — Order: B, C, D, A**
B sets the scene with a full noun ("A spacecraft"). C describes the ongoing action "As it passes." D is the next processing step ("then stitch these... strips"). A is the outcome ("The result").

- Q10: (2) B
- Q11: (3) D — "these thousands of strips" in D refers to the strips the camera snaps in C.
- Q12: (1) A

**Trick:** A result sentence ("The result is...") almost always lands last. The sequence word "then" in D forces it after the action it follows (C). Start from the only sentence that names the subject outright — "A spacecraft."

**Set 5 — Order: B, A, C, D, E**
B introduces "Farmers" (full noun). A gives the contrast "Instead, they store." C qualifies with "This patience, however, depends on cold storage." D covers "Those without it." E is the widening-gap conclusion.

- Q13: (2) B
- Q14: (2) C — "This patience" in C refers to the storing-and-waiting behaviour described in A.
- Q15: (5) E

**Trick:** "Instead" and "This patience, however" are both back-referring connectors, so neither can open — only "Farmers... rarely sell" stands alone. Demonstratives (*this, those*) point backward and lock the middle order; a summarising "gap widens" sentence closes.

### Part B — Rearrangement & Fillers

- **Q16: (1) PQRS** — "decided to postpone the launch until every safety test had been completed to the satisfaction of the regulators." Natural verb-object-clause flow.
- **Q17: (1) PQRS** — "known that the letter she posted would arrive on the very morning of his departure." "would arrive" (Q) must follow its subject "the letter" (P).
- **Q18: (1) PRQS** — "aims to reduce the burden by simplifying the rules... on small businesses" reads awkwardly; correct is "to reduce the burden **on small businesses** by simplifying the rules that govern annual filings," i.e. P-Q-R-S. Answer: (1) PQRS.

**Trick:** Keep the verb next to its own object and let modifier clauses ("that govern...", "of his departure") attach to the noun they describe. Read your chosen order aloud once — the correct chain has no dangling phrase.

- **Q19: (2) resolute** — "Despite the heavy rain" sets up a contrast, so the volunteers stay firm, not hesitant/reluctant. Connector polarity: *despite* demands a positive, determined word.
- **Q20: (3) eloquent** — "even his critics admitted they had been moved" needs a positive quality; only eloquent produces being moved.
- **Q21: (2) circumstantial** — "unwilling to convict on it alone" pairs with weak, indirect evidence; conclusive/overwhelming would justify conviction, the opposite.
- **Q22: (1) recruit** — "struggled to ___ enough staff to meet demand" — a growing firm needs to hire, not dismiss/reject.
- **Q23: (2) cogent** — "persuade the panel" needs a strong, logical argument; vague/frivolous/trivial cannot persuade.
- **Q24: (1) diverted** — traffic is diverted (sent onto another route). Reverted/inverted/averted/converted do not fit road redirection.

**Trick:** Read the *connector* first. "Despite / although / yet" flips the polarity (expect a word opposite to the clue); "because / so / and" keeps the same polarity. Then confirm collocation — traffic *diverts*, evidence is *circumstantial*, staff is *recruited*.

- **Q25: (1) generous ... silent** — "but" flips polarity: praise (positive) vs. saying nothing about risks (negative). Generous praise / silent on risks.
- **Q26: (2) Despite ... confident** — "Despite the setback... pressed on" needs a concessive opener plus a positive attitude ("persistence would pay off" = confident).
- **Q27: (2) raise ... curb** — a scheme lifting rural incomes to reduce city migration. "raise incomes... curb migration" is internally consistent; (5) raise...quicken contradicts the aim.
- **Q28: (2) dense ... untangling** — "tangled sentences" the editor spends weeks on implies difficult prose he must untangle.
- **Q29: (2) lowered ... address** — medicine lowers a fever but fails to treat the infection; "Although" sets the contrast.
- **Q30: (2) cordial ... tirelessly** — "in public... behind closed doors to reach a deal" — outwardly friendly while working hard privately. Hostile/rude contradict reaching a deal.

**Trick:** In double fillers, lock the blank whose clue is strongest, then let the connector decide the second. *But / although / while* means the two blanks pull in opposite directions; *and / so* means they agree. Reject any pair where one word alone breaks the sentence's logic.

### Part C — Cloze

- **Q31: (2) dismissed** — "yet their role has quietly expanded" contrasts with a negative view; libraries are wrongly written off. "celebrated" clashes with "yet."
- **Q32: (1) expanded** — explicitly opposed to "rather than shrunk"; the antonym of shrunk is expanded.
- **Q33: (2) offer** — "many now ___ free internet access... to people" — libraries provide these services.
- **Q34: (2) transformed** — "into a kind of community anchor" needs a change-of-state verb; "transformed... into" is the fixed collocation.
- **Q35: (3) overlook** — critics "who argue everything is online" fail to notice the device gap; overlook = miss. "confirm/prove" would support, not counter, them.
- **Q36: (2) luxury** — "not a ___ but a necessity" — the natural antonym of necessity is luxury.
- **Q37: (3) strips** — "does not merely close a building; it ___ away one of the few public spaces"; "strips away" is the idiom and matches the negative escalation.
- **Q38: (4) wise** — "Any city that values equal opportunity would be ___ to protect it" — protecting it is the sensible act, so wise. (unwise/foolish would need "to abandon it.")

**Trick:** In cloze, ride the connectors. "yet" and "rather than" flag reversals (pick the opposite of the surrounding tone); "not a ___ but a necessity" is a direct antonym slot; and fixed pairs — *transform into*, *strip away* — are collocation locks. Always test your word against the sentence's polarity signal before the neighbouring words.
