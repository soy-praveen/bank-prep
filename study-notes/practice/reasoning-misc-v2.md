## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

### Blood Relations

**Q1. (c) Mother.** The woman is a daughter of her own mother, and her mother has only one daughter — so the "only daughter of my mother" is the woman herself. The man is her son, so she is his mother.
**Trick:** When a female speaker says "the only daughter of my mother," that only daughter is the speaker. Substitute "me" and read the sentence again.

**Q2. (c) Husband.** "Daughter-in-law of my mother" = the wife of Rahul's mother's son. Rahul has no brothers, so his mother's only son is Rahul himself. Thus the girl is Rahul's wife, and Rahul is her husband.
**Trick:** "Daughter-in-law of X" = "wife of X's son." Pin down who that son is; here it collapses to Rahul.

**Q3. (c) Grand-nephew.** A is B's father, so B is A's son. E is A's brother, hence B's uncle. D is B's wife; C is D's son, so C is B's son. C (third generation) relative to E is two levels down a sibling line = grand-nephew.
**Trick:** Draw generations top to bottom. Uncle-to-(nephew's child) is grand-nephew.

**Q4. (a) M × N ÷ T.** M × N: M is the mother of N. N ÷ T: N is the father of T. So M is the mother of T's father = T's grandmother.
**Trick:** For "grandmother," chain a "mother" link into a "parent" link that reaches the target.

**Q5. (d) Mother-in-law.** "Only son of my wife's father" = the wife's brother. The mother of the wife's brother is the wife's mother = Sohan's mother-in-law.
**Trick:** "Only son of wife's father" is just "wife's brother"; his mother is the wife's mother.

**Q6. (a) P × R ÷ Q.** P × R: P is the brother of R. R ÷ Q: R is the mother of Q. So P is the brother of Q's mother = Q's maternal uncle.
**Trick:** Maternal uncle = brother of the mother. Need "brother" chained into "mother" reaching Q.

**Q7. (b) 3.** Genders: F (mother, female), G (male), H (brother of G, male), J (wife, female), I (male), K (sister of I, female). Females = F, J, K = 3.
**Trick:** Tag each member male/female straight from its relation word before counting.

**Q8. (d) Mother.** The woman is a daughter of her mother, and her mother's "only daughter" is therefore the woman herself. The man's mother is the woman, so she is his mother.
**Trick:** Same self-reference rule as Q1 — "only daughter of my mother" said by a female = the speaker.

**Q9. (c) Cousin.** A is B's son. C is B's sister; D is C's son. A and D are children of two siblings (B and C), so they are cousins.
**Trick:** Children of a brother-and-sister pair are cousins.

**Q10. (c) Sister.** "The father of her brother" is simply the lady's father. "The only son of my grandfather" is Ravi's father. Setting them equal, the lady and Ravi share the same father, so the lady is Ravi's sister.
**Trick:** "Father of her brother" reduces to "her father." Equal fathers => siblings.

### Direction Sense

**Q11. (a) 3 km East.** North 5, right = East 3, right = South 5. The 5 North and 5 South cancel, leaving 3 km East.
**Trick:** Equal-and-opposite legs cancel; only the unmatched East leg remains.

**Q12. (b) 5 km.** East 4, then left = North 3. Legs 4 and 3 form a right angle: distance = √(4²+3²) = 5.
**Trick:** The 3-4-5 triple gives the hypotenuse instantly.

**Q13. (c) North, 5 m.** South 12, left = East 5, left = North 12. The two 12 m legs (South then North) cancel, leaving 5 m. He is now facing North.
**Trick:** Track "facing direction" separately from displacement; opposite equal legs cancel, leaving the single side leg (5 m).

**Q14. (a) 8 m North.** North 10, East 6, South 2, West 6. East 6 and West 6 cancel; 10 North − 2 South = 8 North.
**Trick:** Net vertical = 10 − 2 = 8 N; net horizontal = 6 − 6 = 0.

**Q15. (c) South-East.** Take Q at origin. R is East of Q (positive x). S is South of R (same x, lower y). So S is both East and South of Q => South-East.
**Trick:** Assign coordinates from Q; right-and-down of the origin = South-East. (P is a distractor — it plays no role.)

**Q16. (a) 3 m.** North 9, right = East 4, right = South 12, right = West 4. East 4 and West 4 cancel; 9 North − 12 South = 3 South. Distance = 3 m.
**Trick:** Horizontal legs cancel; vertical net 9 − 12 = 3.

**Q17. (c) 13 m.** From the common start, Amit is 12 m North and Sumit is 5 m East — perpendicular paths. Gap = √(12²+5²) = 13.
**Trick:** Perpendicular displacements from one point form a right triangle: 5-12-13 triple.

**Q18. (a) 25 km.** East 20, right = South 15, right = West 20, left = South 10. East 20 and West 20 cancel; South 15 + South 10 = 25 South. Distance = 25 km.
**Trick:** Cancel the equal E/W legs, then add the two southward legs 15 + 10.

### Coding-Decoding

**Q19. (a) DIBJS.** TABLE -> UBCMF is +1 on each letter. CHAIR: C->D, H->I, A->B, I->J, R->S = DIBJS.
**Trick:** Fix the shift from the sample (+1), then apply it uniformly.

**Q20. (a) DTCKP.** POINT -> RQKPV is +2 on each letter (P->R, O->Q, I->K, N->P, T->V). BRAIN: B->D, R->T, A->C, I->K, N->P = DTCKP.
**Trick:** Same idea — confirm +2 from the sample, then shift BRAIN forward by two.

**Q21. (a) 16-5-1-18.** Positions: P=16, E=5, A=1, R=18.
**Trick:** Anchor letters A=1, E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Z=26 and count from the nearest anchor.

**Q22. (a) pa.** "sun rises east" = pa la ka and "sun sets slowly" = pa na ra share only the word "sun" and only the code "pa". So sun = pa.
**Trick:** Intersect the two sentences that both contain "sun"; the shared code is the answer.

**Q23. (b) na.** "moon sets west" = ta na sa and "sun sets slowly" = pa na ra share only "sets" and only "na". So sets = na.
**Trick:** Same intersection method on the two "sets" sentences.

**Q24. (a) DELPQ.** FROST -> COLPQ is −3 on each letter (F->C, R->O, O->L, S->P, T->Q). GHOST: G->D, H->E, O->L, S->P, T->Q = DELPQ.
**Trick:** Read the shift off any clear pair (O->L = −3), then move each letter of GHOST back three.

**Q25. (b) 51243.** Sort TABLE's letters alphabetically: A,B,E,L,T => A=1, B=2, E=3, L=4, T=5. Now read the word in original order: T,A,B,L,E = 5,1,2,4,3 = 51243. (Check with BOARD: A,B,D,O,R = 1,2,3,4,5 => B,O,A,R,D = 24153, matches the given code.)
**Trick:** Alphabetize the word's own letters, number them 1..n, then read the numbers in the word's order.

**Q26. (a) ka.** "green is fresh" = mo ti ka and "fresh and clean" = ka du re share only "fresh" and only "ka". So fresh = ka.
**Trick:** Intersect the two "fresh" sentences => ka.

**Q27. (a) 6286.** From CALCUTTA = 82589662: C=8, A=2, L=5, C=8, U=9, T=6, T=6, A=2. So C=8, A=2, T=6. TACT = T,A,C,T = 6,2,8,6 = 6286.
**Trick:** Build a letter->digit table from the given word, then spell the target letter by letter.

**Q28. (a) EQJ.** Rule: add the letter's position in the word. Check ACE: A+1=B, C+2=E, E+3=H = BEH. Apply to DOG: D+1=E, O+2=Q, G+3=J = EQJ.
**Trick:** +1 to the first letter, +2 to the second, +3 to the third.

**Q29. (a) TUBS.** PLANET -> QMBOFU is +1 on each letter. STAR: S->T, T->U, A->B, R->S = TUBS.
**Trick:** Confirm +1, then shift each letter of STAR forward by one.

**Q30. (d) 2.** "42" = good boy and "43" = good girl share the digit 4, which must be the shared word "good". So in "good boy" (42), the remaining digit 2 = "boy". ("51" = bad boy just confirms boy is a single digit, here 2 does not appear because 51 uses different digits for bad/boy — the pin comes from 42 vs 43.)
**Trick:** Find "good" from the two sentences that share it (4), then the leftover digit of "good boy" is boy = 2.

### Alphanumeric Series

Arrangement with positions:
`1:R 2:4 3:P 4:# 5:M 6:7 7:K 8:% 9:2 10:J 11:D 12:9 13:@ 14:B 15:5 16:W 17:N 18:6 19:$ 20:Q 21:8 22:F 23:© 24:3 25:H 26:T`

**Q31. (b) Two.** A symbol with a number on its left and a letter on its right: @ (pos 13) has 9 on the left and B on the right — yes; $ (pos 19) has 6 on the left and Q on the right — yes. # and % have letters on their left; © has F on its left. So exactly two.
**Trick:** Check only the symbols; for each, test "digit on left AND letter on right."

**Q32. (d) N.** 12th from the left = position 12 = 9. Fifth to its right = position 17 = N.
**Trick:** "kth to the right of the mth from left" = position (m + k) = 12 + 5 = 17.

**Q33. (b) Two.** Numbers with a symbol immediately before them: 2 (pos 9) is preceded by % (pos 8) — yes; 3 (pos 24) is preceded by © (pos 23) — yes. No other number has a symbol on its left. So two.
**Trick:** Scan each digit's left neighbour; count only those that are symbols.

**Q34. (a) 6.** Drop the symbols (# % @ $ ©). Remaining, in order: R 4 P M 7 K 2 J D 9 B 5 W N 6 Q 8 F 3 H T (20 elements). Counting from the right: T, H, 3, F, 8, Q, 6 — the 7th is 6.
**Trick:** After deletion, count straight from the right end; the 7th element is 6.

**Q35. (c) Three.** A letter with a symbol before it and a number after it: M (pos 5) has # before and 7 after — yes; B (pos 14) has @ before and 5 after — yes; Q (pos 20) has $ before and 8 after — yes. So three.
**Trick:** For each symbol, look at the letter just to its right and check whether a digit follows that letter. M, B, Q qualify.

### Order & Ranking

**Q36. (b) 29th.** Position from right = Total − Position from left + 1 = 40 − 12 + 1 = 29.
**Trick:** Right rank = Total − Left rank + 1 (the +1 counts the person themselves).

**Q37. (c) 34.** Total = rank from top + rank from bottom − 1 = 15 + 20 − 1 = 34.
**Trick:** Both ranks count the person once, so subtract 1 for the overlap.

**Q38. (c) 23.** After the swap, Amit sits in Bina's old seat and is 15th from the left, so Bina's original position was 15th from the left. Bina is also 9th from the right. Total = 15 + 9 − 1 = 23.
**Trick:** The interchange transfers Bina's exact position to Amit; then Total = left rank + right rank − 1.

**Q39. (c) R.** Combine the clues: R > P > Q (P is below R, above Q) and Q > S > T (S is below Q, above T). Full order: R > P > Q > S > T. Tallest = R.
**Trick:** Merge the inequalities into one chain and read the top end.

**Q40. (b) 9.** N is 7th from the right in a row of 25, so N is 25 − 7 + 1 = 19th from the left. People between the 9th (M) and the 19th (N) = 19 − 9 − 1 = 9.
**Trick:** Convert both to positions from the same end, then between = difference − 1.
