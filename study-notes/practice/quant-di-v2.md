### Set 1 — Table

The table shows the number of units of two products, **A** and **B**, sold by five stores in a month.

**Q1.** What is the total number of units of Product A sold by all five stores together?
a) 680  b) 700  c) 720  d) 740  e) 760

**Q2.** The total units of Product A sold is in what ratio to the total units of Product B sold?

**Q3.** Which store sold the highest number of units (A and B combined)?

**Q4.** What is the average number of units of Product B sold per store?

## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

### Set 1 — Table

**Q1. Correct: b) 700**
Total A = 120 + 150 + 200 + 90 + 140 = 700.
**Trick:** Pair for easy round sums: (120 + 90) = 210, (150 + 140) = 290, then + 200 = 700. Grouping numbers into hundreds first kills carry-errors.

**Q2. Correct: c) 7:5**
Total A = 700. Total B = 80 + 90 + 160 + 110 + 60 = 500. Ratio 700:500 = 7:5.
**Trick:** Cancel the two trailing zeros immediately — 700:500 becomes 7:5 by inspection. Never carry zeros into ratio-simplification.

**Q3. Correct: c) R**
Combined totals: P = 200, Q = 240, R = 360, S = 200, T = 200. R is highest.
**Trick:** The store leading in BOTH columns usually wins — R tops both A (200) and B (160), so no full addition of the others is needed.

**Q4. Correct: c) 100**
Average B = Total B / 5 = 500 / 5 = 100.
**Trick:** You already found Total B = 500 in Q2 — reuse it. In multi-part DI, carry forward sub-totals rather than re-adding.

**Q5. Correct: c) 45%**
Store S: A = 90, combined = 90 + 110 = 200. Percentage = 90/200 = 45%.
**Trick:** Denominator 200 → multiply numerator by 0.5 and read directly: 90 → 45%. Any "/200" is just "half, as a percent."

### Set 2 — Bar Graph

**Q6. Correct: b) 13,300**
Sum (in hundreds) = 24 + 18 + 36 + 40 + 15 = 133 → 13,300.
**Trick:** Add the hundreds first, attach "00" last. 24+18=42, +36=78, +40=118, +15=133.

**Q7. Correct: d) 50%**
City B: (27 − 18)/18 = 9/18 = 50%.
**Trick:** When the increase equals half the base (9 is half of 18), it's an instant 50% — no long division.

**Q8. Correct: b) B**
Increases: A = 6/24 = 25%, B = 9/18 = 50%, C = negative (36→33), D = 4/40 = 10%, E = 6/15 = 40%. B is highest.
**Trick:** Scan for the largest jump relative to the smallest base. B has a big rise (9) on a small base (18) — the winning combination. Discard C outright (it fell).

**Q9. Correct: c) 15,500**
Sum = 30 + 27 + 33 + 44 + 21 = 155 → 15,500.
**Trick:** 30+27=57, +33=90 (round anchor), +44=134, +21=155. Hunt for a running total that hits a round number (90) to steady the addition.

**Q10. Correct: b) 4:3**
D in 2022 = 40; A in 2023 = 30. Ratio 40:30 = 4:3.
**Trick:** Divide both by the common factor 10 first, then by nothing more needed: 4:3. Strip common zeros before simplifying.

### Set 3 — Pie Chart

Per-degree value = 45,000 / 360 = **Rs 125 per degree**. (Memorise this once for the whole set.)

**Q11. Correct: c) Rs 15,000**
Rent = 120 × 125 = 15,000.
**Trick:** 120 degrees is exactly one-third of 360, so Rent = 45,000 / 3 = 15,000 with zero multiplication.

**Q12. Correct: b) Rs 11,250**
Food = 90 × 125 = 11,250.
**Trick:** 90 degrees = one-quarter of the circle, so Food = 45,000 / 4 = 11,250 instantly.

**Q13. Correct: d) Rs 7,500**
Rent (15,000) − Savings (60 × 125 = 7,500) = 7,500.
**Trick:** Work with the angle gap: Rent − Savings = (120 − 60) degrees = 60 degrees = 60 × 125 = 7,500. Differences on a pie are just angle differences times the per-degree rate.

**Q14. Correct: b) 12.5%**
Transport share = 45/360 = 12.5%.
**Trick:** Percentage on a pie = angle / 360. 45 is one-eighth of 360, and 1/8 = 12.5%. Learn the eighths (12.5, 25, 37.5...).

**Q15. Correct: c) Rs 9,000**
Savings = 7,500; +20% = 7,500 × 1.2 = 9,000.
**Trick:** 20% of 7,500 is 1,500 (just 7,500 / 5). Add it back: 9,000. Dividing by 5 beats multiplying by 0.2.

### Set 4 — Line Graph

**Q16. Correct: c) 1,500**
250 + 300 + 280 + 350 + 320 = 1,500.
**Trick:** Pair to round hundreds: (250 + 350) = 600, (300 + 320) = 620, wait—simpler: (250+350)=600, (280+320)=600, +300 = 1,500. Two clean 600s plus the middle value.

**Q17. Correct: c) 300**
Average = 1,500 / 5 = 300.
**Trick:** Reuse the Q16 total. Also note the values sit symmetrically around 300, so 300 is the natural average — a quick sanity check.

**Q18. Correct: c) 40%**
(350 − 250)/250 = 100/250 = 40%.
**Trick:** 100/250 = 2/5 = 40%. Convert to a simple fraction before percentaging.

**Q19. Correct: b) 15:16**
Feb : May = 300 : 320. Divide both by 20 → 15:16.
**Trick:** Common factor 20 clears it fast: 300/20 = 15, 320/20 = 16. Look for the largest obvious common factor, not just 2s.

**Q20. Correct: c) 28%**
(320 − 250)/250 = 70/250 = 28%.
**Trick:** 70/250 = 28/100 (multiply top and bottom by 0.4, or note 250 × 4 = 1000 so ×4 both: 280/1000 = 28%). Scaling the denominator to 1000 gives the percentage directly.

### Set 5 — Caselet

Boys : Girls = 4:3 of 840 → 7 parts = 840, so 1 part = 120. Boys = 480, Girls = 360.

**Q21. Correct: c) 480**
Boys = 4 × 120 = 480.
**Trick:** Total parts (4 + 3 = 7) divide the total once: 840 / 7 = 120 per part. Set the "per part" value first, then everything else is a multiplication.

**Q22. Correct: d) 264**
Boys with glasses = 25% of 480 = 120. Girls with glasses = 40% of 360 = 144. Total = 264.
**Trick:** 25% = divide by 4 (480/4 = 120); 40% = 0.4 (360 × 0.4 = 144). Use the "friendly form" of each percent.

**Q23. Correct: d) 360**
Boys without glasses = 480 − 120 = 360 (i.e. 75% of 480).
**Trick:** 75% of 480 = three-quarters = 360 directly, no subtraction needed.

**Q24. Correct: b) 5:6**
Boys-with : Girls-with = 120 : 144. Divide both by 24 → 5:6.
**Trick:** Both are divisible by 24 (120 = 24×5, 144 = 24×6). Spot the shared factor via the times-tables rather than reducing step by step.

**Q25. Correct: c) 60%**
Girls without glasses = 60% of girls (since 40% wear them). = 100% − 40%.
**Trick:** "Not X%" is just the complement — 100 − 40 = 60%. No headcount needed at all.

### Set 6 — Mixed

Online amounts: X = 60% of 400 = 240; Y = 40% of 500 = 200; Z = 60% of 300 = 180.

**Q26. Correct: d) 620**
240 + 200 + 180 = 620.
**Trick:** Compute each online figure as (percent × total) using friendly fractions: 60% = 3/5, 40% = 2/5. 400×3/5 = 240, 500×2/5 = 200, 300×3/5 = 180.

**Q27. Correct: d) 300**
Offline Y = 500 − 200 = 300 (i.e. 60% of 500).
**Trick:** Offline share = 100% − online share = 60% of 500 = 300 straight.

**Q28. Correct: b) 4:3**
Online X : Online Z = 240 : 180. Divide by 60 → 4:3.
**Trick:** Both share factor 60 (240 = 60×4, 180 = 60×3). Since X and Z have the same online rate (60%), the ratio is just their totals 400:300 = 4:3 — skip computing the online amounts entirely.

**Q29. Correct: d) 1,200**
400 + 500 + 300 = 1,200.
**Trick:** Totals column adds cleanly to a round 1,200 — no channel split needed here; read the "Total Sales" column directly.

**Q30. Correct: c) 33.33%**
(240 − 180)/180 = 60/180 = 1/3 = 33.33%.
**Trick:** 60 is one-third of 180, so the excess is 33.33% by inspection. Whenever the gap equals a third of the base, answer 33.33% without dividing.
