# Puzzles & Seating — Practice (Vol. 3)

SBI PO Prelims level (moderate). Six fresh sets, 30 questions in all. Each set has a single, unique arrangement. Read the setup carefully — direction conventions are stated where they matter.

---

## Questions

### Set 1 — Linear row, both directions (8 persons)

Eight persons — A, B, C, D, E, F, G and H — sit in a straight row. Positions are counted 1 to 8 from the left end (as we look at the row). Each person faces either North or South. For a person facing **North**, their right hand points toward the higher-numbered (right) end; for a person facing **South**, their right hand points toward the lower-numbered (left) end.

Clues:
1. Only two persons sit to the left of A (A is third from the left).
2. H sits third from the right end.
3. D sits at the extreme right end.
4. Four of them face North and the remaining four face South.
5. E sits immediately to the right of A.
6. A and E face opposite directions.
7. F faces South.
8. Exactly one person sits between F and H, and that person is C.
9. The person at the extreme left end faces South.
10. Both persons sitting at the extreme ends face the same direction.
11. B sits immediately to the right of H.
12. C faces the same direction as E.
13. H faces the same direction as B.

**Q1.** How many persons sit between C and D?
(a) One  (b) Two  (c) Three  (d) Four  (e) None

**Q2.** Who sits immediately to the right of E?
(a) F  (b) C  (c) A  (d) G  (e) H

**Q3.** Which of the following pairs sits at the two extreme ends of the row?
(a) G and F  (b) E and D  (c) G and D  (d) C and B  (e) A and H

**Q4.** How many persons face South?
(a) Three  (b) Four  (c) Five  (d) Two  (e) Six

**Q5.** Who sits third to the left of B?
(a) C  (b) H  (c) A  (d) F  (e) E

### Set 2 — Circular table, facing centre (8 persons)

Eight persons — P, Q, R, S, T, U, V and W — sit around a circular table, all **facing the centre**. (Standard convention: when a person faces the centre, "immediate right" is the neighbour on the anticlockwise side and "immediate left" is the neighbour on the clockwise side.)

Clues:
1. U sits to the immediate left of P.
2. Q sits to the immediate left of U.
3. Only one person sits between Q and V.
4. S sits between Q and V.
5. V sits exactly opposite P.
6. R sits to the immediate right of P.
7. W sits to the immediate right of R, and T sits to the immediate right of W.

**Q1.** Who sits immediately to the right of V?
(a) T  (b) S  (c) Q  (d) R  (e) W

**Q2.** Who sits exactly opposite W?
(a) P  (b) U  (c) Q  (d) S  (e) T

**Q3.** Who sits second to the left of P?
(a) U  (b) Q  (c) S  (d) R  (e) V

**Q4.** In each of the following pairs "X - Y", X sits immediately to the right of Y. Four of them follow this rule. Which one does NOT?
(a) U - Q  (b) P - U  (c) R - P  (d) S - V  (e) T - R

**Q5.** Who are the immediate neighbours of Q?
(a) P and S  (b) U and S  (c) U and V  (d) S and V  (e) P and U

### Set 3 — Floors and fruits (double variable)

An eight-storey building has floors numbered 1 (bottom) to 8 (top). Eight persons — A, B, C, D, E, F, G and H — live on separate floors, one per floor. Each likes a different fruit among Apple, Banana, Cherry, Date, Fig, Grape, Kiwi and Lemon.

Clues:
1. A lives on the lowest floor.
2. E lives on floor 2.
3. F lives on floor 4.
4. G lives on the topmost floor.
5. C lives immediately above E.
6. B lives immediately above F.
7. D lives immediately above B.
8. A likes Fig and G likes Kiwi.
9. E likes Date.
10. H likes Lemon.
11. C likes Banana.
12. The one who likes Cherry lives immediately above the one who likes Banana.
13. The one who likes Grape lives immediately above the one who likes Apple.

**Q1.** Who lives on floor 5?
(a) D  (b) H  (c) B  (d) F  (e) C

**Q2.** Which fruit does D like?
(a) Apple  (b) Grape  (c) Lemon  (d) Cherry  (e) Kiwi

**Q3.** Who lives immediately above the person who likes Cherry?
(a) D  (b) H  (c) F  (d) B  (e) G

**Q4.** How many persons live between the one who likes Fig and the one who likes Lemon?
(a) Four  (b) Five  (c) Three  (d) Six  (e) Two

**Q5.** Which of the following combinations (person - fruit - floor) is correct?
(a) C - Cherry - Floor 3  (b) D - Grape - Floor 6  (c) B - Apple - Floor 6  (d) F - Banana - Floor 4  (e) H - Kiwi - Floor 7

### Set 4 — Comparison / ranking by weight (7 persons)

Seven persons — P, Q, R, S, T, U and V — have different weights.

Clues:
1. P is heavier than Q, who is heavier than R.
2. S is heavier than exactly two of the others.
3. Only one person is heavier than T.
4. U is neither the heaviest nor the lightest.
5. R is heavier than V.
6. P is heavier than T.
7. Exactly three persons lie between Q and V in the weight order.
8. R is heavier than U.

**Q1.** Who is the heaviest?
(a) T  (b) P  (c) Q  (d) S  (e) R

**Q2.** Who is the lightest?
(a) U  (b) S  (c) V  (d) R  (e) Q

**Q3.** How many persons are heavier than R?
(a) Two  (b) Three  (c) Four  (d) One  (e) Five

**Q4.** Who is just heavier than S?
(a) Q  (b) U  (c) R  (d) T  (e) V

**Q5.** Who are, respectively, the 2nd heaviest and the 6th heaviest?
(a) Q and U  (b) T and U  (c) T and S  (d) P and V  (e) R and U

### Set 5 — Month puzzle (7 persons)

Seven persons — L, M, N, O, P, Q and R — each attend a workshop in a different month among seven consecutive months from February to August (one person per month).

Clues:
1. L attends in April.
2. M attends in February.
3. P attends in August.
4. N attends after L.
5. O attends in the month immediately after N.
6. Q attends in the month immediately before L.
7. R attends after O.

**Q1.** Who attends in the earliest month?
(a) Q  (b) L  (c) M  (d) P  (e) N

**Q2.** How many persons attend between L and R (exclusive)?
(a) One  (b) Two  (c) Three  (d) Four  (e) None

**Q3.** Who attends in June?
(a) N  (b) O  (c) R  (d) L  (e) Q

**Q4.** If everyone is ordered from the earliest month to the latest, who is exactly in the middle (4th)?
(a) L  (b) N  (c) O  (d) Q  (e) R

**Q5.** Who attends in the month immediately before P?
(a) O  (b) N  (c) R  (d) L  (e) M

### Set 6 — Blood relations (family of 8)

A family has exactly eight members — A, B, C, D, E, F, G and H — spanning three generations, and it contains exactly three married couples.

Clues:
1. A is the husband of B.
2. B is the mother of C.
3. C is the brother of D.
4. E is the daughter-in-law of B.
5. F is the son-in-law of A.
6. G is the son of E.
7. D is the mother of H.
8. H is the daughter of F.

**Q1.** How is G related to A?
(a) Son  (b) Grandson  (c) Nephew  (d) Brother  (e) Grandfather

**Q2.** How is E related to D?
(a) Sister  (b) Mother  (c) Sister-in-law  (d) Aunt  (e) Daughter

**Q3.** How is F related to G?
(a) Father  (b) Brother  (c) Uncle  (d) Grandfather  (e) Cousin

**Q4.** How is H related to C?
(a) Daughter  (b) Niece  (c) Sister  (d) Cousin  (e) Aunt

**Q5.** How many male members are there in the family?
(a) Three  (b) Four  (c) Five  (d) Six  (e) Two

## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

### Set 1 — solved arrangement

Left (1) to right (8), with facing in brackets:

| Pos | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|-----|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Person | G | E | A | F | C | H | B | D |
| Faces | S | N | S | S | N | N | N | S |

How to reach it: A is 3rd from left (pos 3), H is 3rd from right (pos 6), D at the right end (pos 8). Clue 8 puts F and H with exactly one person between and that person C, so F=4, C=5. Clue 5 (E immediately right of A) — if A faced North, E would be at pos 4, but that is F; so A faces South and E sits at pos 2. Clue 6 makes E face North; clue 12 makes C face North. Positions 1 and 7 remain for G and B. Clue 11 (B immediately right of H): H must face North to place B at pos 7, so B=7 and G=1. Clue 9 gives G (left end) South, clue 10 gives D (right end) South. Clue 13 makes B face North. Count check: North = E, C, H, B (four); South = G, A, F, D (four) — matches clue 4.

- **Q1 — (b) Two.** C is at 5, D at 8; positions 6 and 7 (H, B) lie between. **Trick:** Between-count = (difference of positions) − 1 = 8 − 5 − 1 = 2. Ignore facing for "how many between".
- **Q2 — (c) A.** E is at pos 2 facing North, so its right is pos 3 = A. **Trick:** North-facer's right = next higher position.
- **Q3 — (c) G and D.** Ends are pos 1 (G) and pos 8 (D).
- **Q4 — (b) Four.** G, A, F, D face South. **Trick:** Clue 4 already fixes it as four — no need to build the whole row for this one.
- **Q5 — (d) F.** B is at pos 7 facing North, so its left is the lower-numbered side; third to the left = pos 7 − 3 = pos 4 = F. **Trick:** For a North-facer, "to the left" means subtract positions.

### Set 2 — solved arrangement

Going clockwise, all facing the centre: **P, U, Q, S, V, T, W, R** (then back to P).

How to reach it: fix P. Clue 1 puts U on P's immediate left (clockwise neighbour); clue 2 puts Q on U's immediate left. Clue 6 puts R on P's immediate right (anticlockwise neighbour); clue 7 chains W then T anticlockwise from R. Clue 5 puts V opposite P. Clues 3 and 4 confirm S sits between Q and V (the single seat separating them). This fills all eight seats consistently.

- **Q1 — (b) S.** V's immediate right is the anticlockwise neighbour = S. **Trick:** Facing centre, "right" moves anticlockwise — the opposite of what a floor plan feels like.
- **Q2 — (c) Q.** The person opposite W (4 seats away) is Q. **Trick:** Opposite = seat + 4 in an 8-seat circle.
- **Q3 — (b) Q.** Second to P's left (clockwise) is U then Q.
- **Q4 — (e) T - R.** R's immediate right is W, not T; the other four pairs are genuine "immediate right" pairs. **Trick:** Odd-one-out seating items are usually a broken adjacency — test each pair against the ring.
- **Q5 — (b) U and S.** Q's neighbours are U (left) and S (right).

### Set 3 — solved arrangement

Floor 8 (top) down to floor 1 (bottom):

| Floor | Person | Fruit |
|-------|--------|-------|
| 8 | G | Kiwi |
| 7 | H | Lemon |
| 6 | D | Grape |
| 5 | B | Apple |
| 4 | F | Cherry |
| 3 | C | Banana |
| 2 | E | Date |
| 1 | A | Fig |

How to reach it: A=1, E=2, F=4, G=8 are given. Clue 5 puts C on floor 3 (above E); clue 6 puts B on floor 5 (above F); clue 7 puts D on floor 6 (above B); floor 7 is then H. Fruits: A=Fig, G=Kiwi, E=Date, H=Lemon, C=Banana are fixed. Clue 12 (Cherry immediately above Banana) makes floor 4 = Cherry (F). The remaining Apple and Grape go to floors 5 and 6; clue 13 (Grape immediately above Apple) forces Apple=5 (B), Grape=6 (D).

- **Q1 — (c) B.** Floor 5 is B.
- **Q2 — (b) Grape.** D on floor 6 likes Grape.
- **Q3 — (d) B.** Cherry is on floor 4 (F); immediately above is floor 5 = B. **Trick:** "Immediately above" means +1 floor — do not confuse with the fruit's own lover.
- **Q4 — (b) Five.** Fig = floor 1, Lemon = floor 7; floors 2–6 give five persons. **Trick:** Between-count = 7 − 1 − 1 = 5.
- **Q5 — (b) D - Grape - Floor 6.** Only this triple matches the grid.

### Set 4 — solved order

Heaviest to lightest: **P > T > Q > R > S > U > V.**

How to reach it: clue 3 makes T the 2nd heaviest; clue 6 (P heavier than T) makes P the heaviest. Clue 1 keeps P > Q > R. Clue 2 fixes S at 5th (heavier than exactly two = U, V). Clue 7 (three persons between Q and V) with Q high forces V lightest and Q 3rd. Clues 5 and 8 (R heavier than both V and U) place R 4th, U 6th.

- **Q1 — (b) P.** **Trick:** "Only one heavier than T" ranks T second; combined with "P heavier than T", P must top the list.
- **Q2 — (c) V.** V is at the bottom.
- **Q3 — (b) Three.** P, T, Q are heavier than R.
- **Q4 — (c) R.** Just above S (5th) is R (4th). **Trick:** "Just heavier than X" = the person exactly one rank above X.
- **Q5 — (b) T and U.** 2nd heaviest = T, 6th heaviest = U.

### Set 5 — solved order

Earliest to latest: **M (Feb) -> Q (Mar) -> L (Apr) -> N (May) -> O (Jun) -> R (Jul) -> P (Aug).**

How to reach it: L = April, M = February, P = August fixed. Clue 6 puts Q in March (just before April). N is after April (clue 4) and O is the very next month (clue 5); R is after O (clue 7). The only way to fit N, O, R into May, June, July with N before O before... is N = May, O = June, R = July (if N = June, O = July then R would need August, already P's — impossible).

- **Q1 — (c) M.** February is the earliest of the seven months.
- **Q2 — (b) Two.** Between April (L) and July (R) sit May (N) and June (O). **Trick:** Convert names to month numbers and subtract: 7 − 4 − 1 = 2.
- **Q3 — (b) O.** June belongs to O.
- **Q4 — (b) N.** With 7 people, the 4th is the middle = May = N.
- **Q5 — (c) R.** August is P; the month just before is July = R.

### Set 6 — solved family tree

Three generations:
- Generation 1: **A (M)** married to **B (F)**.
- Generation 2: their children **C (M)** and **D (F)**. C is married to **E (F)**; D is married to **F (M)**.
- Generation 3: **G (M)**, son of C and E; **H (F)**, daughter of D and F.

Males: A, C, F, G. Females: B, D, E, H.

How to reach it: A husband of B (A male, B female). C brother of D (C male). E is B's daughter-in-law, so E (female) married B's son C. F is A's son-in-law, so F (male) married A's daughter — since C is male, the daughter is D, so D is female and married F. G (son of E) is male; H (daughter of F, also daughter of D) is female.

- **Q1 — (b) Grandson.** G is the son of C, who is the son of A; so G is A's grandson. **Trick:** Climb generations — child of a child = grandchild; gender of G (son) makes it grandson.
- **Q2 — (c) Sister-in-law.** E is the wife of C, and C is D's brother, so E is D's sister-in-law.
- **Q3 — (c) Uncle.** F is the husband of D, and D is the sister of G's father C; so F is G's uncle. **Trick:** Spouse of a parent's sibling = uncle/aunt.
- **Q4 — (b) Niece.** H is the daughter of D, and C is D's brother; so H is C's niece.
- **Q5 — (b) Four.** Males are A, C, F and G. **Trick:** Nail down every member's gender from marriage/parent clues first — "husband/wife", "son/daughter", "brother/sister" each fix a gender.
