# Quant Trap-Busters — Practice

SBI PO Prelims level. Every question below is engineered around a specific, common mistake. The goal is not just to get the answer — it is to *feel* the pull of the wrong answer and learn to resist it. Attempt each question, commit to an option, then check the Answer Key where each solution names the trap you were meant to fall into.

Traps covered: "% of" vs "% more/less than" (base confusion), average-vs-sum, reading the wrong table cell, unit traps (thousands/lakhs), successive percentage change, ratio-vs-fraction confusion, over-rounding in approximation, "what % more" reference, CI-vs-SI, and profit% base (CP not SP).

### Set J — Profit % is on CP, not SP

**37.** An article is bought for Rs 400 and sold for Rs 500. Find the profit percent.
a) 25%  b) 20%  c) 15%  d) 30%  e) 24%

**38.** By selling an article for Rs 600, a shopkeeper gains 20%. Find the cost price.
a) 500  b) 480  c) 520  d) 450  e) 720

**39.** A trader calculates his profit as 25% of the selling price. What is his profit percent on cost?
a) 33.33%  b) 25%  c) 20%  d) 30%  e) 50%

**40.** An item costing Rs 800 is sold at a loss of 15%. Find the selling price.
a) 680  b) 941  c) 920  d) 720  e) 700

## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

**1. Answer: (a) 32,000.**
*Method:* "A is 25% more than B" means A = B × 1.25. So B = 40,000 / 1.25 = **32,000**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 30,000.* People subtract 25% *of A*: 40,000 − 10,000 = 30,000. But 25% of A is not the same as 25% of B.
**Trick:** In "more/less than", the quantity after "than" is the base = 100%. Set B = 100, A = 125, so B = A × 100/125. To reverse a % increase you divide by 1.25 — never subtract the same %.

**2. Answer: (a) 56,000.**
*Method:* "X is 30% less than Y" → X = Y × 0.70 = 80,000 × 0.70 = **56,000**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 1,14,286.* People read it as "Y is 30% less than X" and divide: 80,000 / 0.70 = 1,14,286.
**Trick:** The subject of the sentence (X) is what you are solving for; the object after "than" (Y) is the base. X less than Y → multiply Y directly by 0.70.

**3. Answer: (a) 600.**
*Method:* If 20% failed, 80% passed. 80% of the total = 480 → total = 480 / 0.80 = **600**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 576.* People take 20% of the passed count (0.20 × 480 = 96) and add: 480 + 96 = 576, treating "passed" as the base.
**Trick:** The percentage is always of the TOTAL that appeared, not of a sub-group. 480 is 80% of the whole — divide, don't add a slice of itself.

**4. Answer: (a) 300.**
*Method:* Number = 210 / 0.35 = 600. Then 50% of 600 = **300**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 105.* People apply 50% straight to 210, giving 105.
**Trick:** When two different percentages of the *same whole* are involved, find the whole first, then take the percentage asked. Never chain one percentage onto another percentage's result.

**5. Answer: (a) 130.**
*Method:* Sum = average × count = 26 × 5 = **130**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 26.* Under time pressure people re-read "average 26" and tick 26, having computed nothing.
**Trick:** Underline the exact word asked — "total/sum" vs "average". Sum = average × count.

**6. Answer: (a) 60.**
*Method:* Total of 6 = 40 × 6 = 240. First 4 = 45 × 4 = 180. Last 2 sum = 240 − 180 = **60**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 30.* People finish by dividing 60 by 2 to get the *average* of the last two — but the question asked for the SUM.
**Trick:** Read the last word of the question again before ticking. Here the work ends at the subtraction; do not take the extra "÷ 2" step.

**7. Answer: (a) 40.**
*Method:* Average = sum / count = 320 / 8 = **40**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 320.* People restate the given sum, or multiply again (320 × 8).
**Trick:** Average = sum ÷ count. If the sum is given, your job is to divide, not to repeat the number.

**8. Answer: (a) 28.**
*Method:* Five consecutive even numbers with average 24: the average is the middle term, so they are 20, 22, 24, 26, 28. Largest = **28**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 24.* People stop at the average (the middle number) instead of stepping out to the largest.
**Trick:** For an odd count of equally-spaced numbers the average = middle term. After finding it, step out (+2, +2) to reach the specific term asked.

**9. Answer: (a) 90.**
*Method:* Row C, column 2022 → **90**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 140.* That is C in 2023 — a slide into the wrong column.
**Trick:** Anchor the row with one finger, then move straight down the correct year's column. Confirm both coordinates before reading.

**10. Answer: (a) 30.**
*Method:* Item A: 150 − 120 = **30**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 150.* People read off the 2023 value as if it were the increase.
**Trick:** "Increase" = later value − earlier value, staying in the same row. An increase can never equal a raw cell value.

**11. Answer: (a) 380.**
*Method:* Item B: 200 + 180 = **380**.
*Common wrong answer: (c) 180.* People grab a single year's figure, or slip to a neighbouring row.
**Trick:** "Over both years" = sum along that item's row. Read both cells of row B, add them.

**12. Answer: (a) 60.**
*Method:* 2022 total = 120 + 200 + 90 + 160 = 570. 2023 total = 150 + 180 + 140 + 160 = 630. Difference = 630 − 570 = **60**.
*Common wrong answer: (d) 630.* People report just the 2023 column total, forgetting the subtraction, or drop one item while adding.
**Trick:** Add all four rows for each column fully (count your terms — there should be 4), then subtract. Missing one item is the classic slip.

**13. Answer: (a) 7 lakh.**
*Method:* 4.5 lakh = 4,50,000; 250 thousand = 2,50,000. Sum = 7,00,000 = **7 lakh**.
*Common wrong answer: (c) 29.5 lakh.* People misread 250 thousand as 25 lakh: 4.5 + 25 = 29.5.
**Trick:** Convert everything to ONE unit before adding. 1 lakh = 100 thousand, so 250 thousand = 2.5 lakh, not 25 lakh.

**14. Answer: (a) 45,000.**
*Method:* "In thousands" → multiply by 1,000: 45 × 1,000 = **45,000**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 45,00,000.* People multiply by 1,00,000 as if the scale were "in lakhs".
**Trick:** Read the scale label exactly. "In thousands" = × 1,000 (three zeros), "in lakhs" = × 1,00,000 (five zeros).

**15. Answer: (a) 1.35 lakh.**
*Method:* 1.2 lakh = 1,20,000; + 15,000 = 1,35,000 = **1.35 lakh**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 2.7 lakh.* People read 15,000 as 1.5 lakh and add 1.2 + 1.5.
**Trick:** 15,000 = 0.15 lakh (divide by 1,00,000), not 1.5 lakh. Convert before adding.

**16. Answer: (a) X, by 4 crore.**
*Method:* X = 12 crore = 1,200 lakh. Y = 800 lakh. X − Y = 400 lakh = **4 crore**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) Y, by 788 crore.* People compare the bare numbers 12 vs 800 and conclude Y is far larger.
**Trick:** Never compare across different units. Convert both to the same unit (1 crore = 100 lakh) first, then compare.

**17. Answer: (a) 4% decrease.**
*Method:* Net factor = 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → **4% decrease**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) No change.* People assume +20% and −20% cancel out.
**Trick:** Successive percentages don't add. Net% = a + b + ab/100 = 20 − 20 − 400/100 = −4%. The decrease acts on the already-inflated price, so you lose more than you gained.

**18. Answer: (a) 32%.**
*Method:* 1.10 × 1.20 = 1.32 → **32% increase**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 30%.* People simply add 10 + 20.
**Trick:** Net% = 10 + 20 + (10 × 20)/100 = 32%. The extra 2% is the second rise acting on the first rise.

**19. Answer: (a) 6.25% decrease.**
*Method:* 0.75 × 1.25 = 0.9375 → **6.25% decrease**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) No change.* People think −25% then +25% returns to start.
**Trick:** Equal up-and-down percentages ALWAYS give a net decrease of (x²/100)% = 25²/100 = 6.25%, regardless of order.

**20. Answer: (a) 17% increase.**
*Method:* Area factor = 1.30 × 0.90 = 1.17 → **17% increase**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 20% increase.* People just do 30 − 10.
**Trick:** For area, multiply the two dimension factors. Net% = 30 − 10 − (30 × 10)/100 = 30 − 10 − 3 = 17%.

**21. Answer: (a) 3/5.**
*Method:* Fraction of whole = part / (sum of parts) = 3 / (3 + 2) = **3/5**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 3/2.* People write the ratio itself as the fraction.
**Trick:** A ratio 3:2 compares two parts; a fraction of the WHOLE needs the total in the denominator: 3/(3+2).

**22. Answer: (a) 16.**
*Method:* Parts = 5 + 7 = 12; each part = 96/12 = 8. A = 40, B = 56. B − A = **16** (also = (7−5) × 8 = 2 × 8).
*Common wrong answer: (b) 56.* People report B's full share instead of the difference asked.
**Trick:** "How much more" = difference of shares = (difference in ratio terms) × (value of one part), not either share by itself.

**23. Answer: (a) 25%.**
*Method:* Zinc fraction = 1 / (3 + 1) = 1/4 = **25%**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 33.33%.* People compute 1/3, using a ratio term as a fraction.
**Trick:** Percentage of a component = its term / (total of all terms) = 1/4, not 1/3.

**24. Answer: (a) 8:15.**
*Method:* Make B common. A:B = 2:3 = 8:12; B:C = 4:5 = 12:15. So A:C = **8:15**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 2:5.* People just read the outer terms (2 from the first, 5 from the second).
**Trick:** Before combining chained ratios, scale so the common term (B) matches — here LCM(3,4) = 12 — then read off A and C.

**25. Answer: (a) 400.**
*Method:* 0.336 ≈ 1/3, so 1197 × 0.336 ≈ 1197/3 ≈ 402. Nearest option = **400**. (Exact: 402.2.)
*Common wrong answer: (b) 360.* People over-round 0.336 down to 0.3: 1197 × 0.3 = 359.
**Trick:** Don't over-round. 0.336 is much closer to 1/3 (0.333) than to 0.3; rounding to 0.3 throws away about 10% of the value.

**26. Answer: (a) 350.**
*Method:* 66.6% ≈ 2/3 of 450 = 300; 33.3% ≈ 1/3 of 150 = 50; total = **350**. (Exact: 349.5.)
*Common wrong answer: (b) 360.* People round 66.6% up to 70% and 33.3% down to 30%: 315 + 45 = 360.
**Trick:** Use exact fraction equivalents (66.6% = 2/3, 33.3% = 1/3), not lazy 70%/30% roundings.

**27. Answer: (a) 220.**
*Method:* 14.95% ≈ 15% of 800 = 120; 24.9% ≈ 25% of 400 = 100; total = **220**. (Exact: 219.2.)
*Common wrong answer: (b) 200.* People round 24.9% down to 20% (100 → 80), giving 200.
**Trick:** Round to the NEAREST clean value. 24.9% is essentially 25% (= 1/4), not 20%. Rounding to the far side of a natural fraction is the error.

**28. Answer: (a) 30.**
*Method:* 899.8 / 29.95 ≈ 900 / 30 = **30**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 300.* A decimal-place slip: dividing 900 by 3 instead of 30.
**Trick:** Keep track of magnitude. 29.95 ≈ 30 (two significant figures), not 3. A quick sanity check: 30 × 30 = 900, so the answer must be near 30.

**29. Answer: (a) 25%.**
*Method:* (150 − 120) / 120 × 100 = 30/120 × 100 = **25%**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 20%.* People divide the difference by A (150): 30/150 = 20%.
**Trick:** "% more than B" → the base is whatever follows "than". Divide the difference by B (the reference), not by the bigger number.

**30. Answer: (a) 20%.**
*Method:* (150 − 120) / 150 × 100 = 30/150 × 100 = **20%**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 25%.* People divide by 120 out of habit from Q29.
**Trick:** "% less than A" → base is A (150). The reference is always the quantity after "than" — it flips between this question and the previous one, so read carefully.

**31. Answer: (a) 25%.**
*Method:* (50 − 40) / 40 × 100 = 10/40 × 100 = **25%**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 20%.* People divide by the score 50: 10/50 = 20%.
**Trick:** "Above the pass mark" makes the pass mark (40) the reference. Divide the excess by the benchmark you are measuring against, not by the achieved value.

**32. Answer: (a) 25% and 20%.**
*Method:* Rise = 20/80 × 100 = 25% (base = old price 80). Fall = 20/100 × 100 = 20% (base = 100, the price it fell from). So **25% rise, 20% fall**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) both 20%.* People assume the same rupee change (Rs 20) means the same percentage both ways.
**Trick:** Percentage always depends on the starting point. Going up, base is 80; coming down, base is 100. Different bases → different percentages, even for the same absolute change.

**33. Answer: (a) 2,000.**
*Method:* SI = P × R × T / 100 = 10,000 × 10 × 2 / 100 = **2,000**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 2,100.* People compute compound interest by mistake.
**Trick:** Simple interest is flat on the original principal every year — no compounding. Just P × R × T / 100.

**34. Answer: (a) 2,100.**
*Method:* Amount = 10,000 × (1.1)² = 12,100. CI = 12,100 − 10,000 = **2,100**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 2,000.* People compute simple interest instead.
**Trick:** CI = P[(1 + R/100)^T − 1]. The extra Rs 100 over the SI of 2,000 is the interest earned on the first year's interest.

**35. Answer: (a) 5,000.**
*Method:* For 2 years, CI − SI = P × (R/100)² = P × (0.1)² = 0.01P. So 0.01P = 50 → P = **5,000**. (Check: SI = 1,000, CI = 1,050, difference = 50.)
*Common wrong answer: (b) 250.* People misuse the SI formula (50 = P × 10 × 2 / 100 → P = 250).
**Trick:** For a 2-year difference, use the shortcut CI − SI = P(R/100)². The difference is tiny relative to P, so the sum must be large.

**36. Answer: (a) 15.**
*Method:* At SI, doubling means the interest equals P, earned in 5 years. To become 4 times, interest must equal 3P → time = 3 × 5 = **15 years**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 10.* People think "4 times = double the doubling", so 2 × 5 = 10 — that would be compound-style intuition.
**Trick:** Simple interest grows the *amount* linearly. 2× means +1P (5 yr); 3× means +2P (10 yr); 4× means +3P (15 yr). Count the added principals, not the multiples.

**37. Answer: (a) 25%.**
*Method:* Profit = 500 − 400 = 100. Profit% = 100/400 × 100 = **25%**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 20%.* People divide by the selling price 500: 100/500 = 20%.
**Trick:** Profit% is ALWAYS on cost price: profit / CP × 100. The SP is never the base.

**38. Answer: (a) 500.**
*Method:* SP = CP × 1.20 → CP = 600 / 1.20 = **500**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 480.* People take 20% off the SP: 600 − 20% of 600 = 480 — but the 20% gain is on CP, not SP.
**Trick:** To undo a gain%, divide the SP by (1 + gain%), don't subtract the gain% of the SP.

**39. Answer: (a) 33.33%.**
*Method:* Profit = 25% of SP → CP = SP − 0.25 SP = 0.75 SP. Profit% on cost = 0.25 SP / 0.75 SP × 100 = 1/3 = **33.33%**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 25%.* People report the given 25% directly, ignoring that it is measured on SP.
**Trick:** A profit stated on SP must be converted to CP base. 25% of SP = 33⅓% of CP (since CP is the smaller 75% slice).

**40. Answer: (a) 680.**
*Method:* Loss% is on CP: SP = 800 × (1 − 0.15) = 800 × 0.85 = **680**.
*Common wrong answer: (b) 941.* People divide by 0.85 (800 / 0.85 = 941) instead of multiplying — reversing the operation.
**Trick:** Loss/gain% is a fraction OF the cost price, so multiply CP by (1 − loss%). Divide only when you are given the SP and need the CP.
