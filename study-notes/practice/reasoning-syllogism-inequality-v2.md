# Syllogism & Inequality — Practice (Vol. 2)

SBI PO Prelims level (moderate). 40 questions: Q1–Q20 Syllogism, Q21–Q40 Inequality (Q21–Q30 direct, Q31–Q40 coded).

**Options (all questions):**
a) Only conclusion I follows
b) Only conclusion II follows
c) Either I or II follows
d) Neither I nor II follows
e) Both I and II follow

## Questions

### Inequality — Coded (Q31–Q40)

In the following questions the symbols are used with these meanings:

- `A # B` → A is **not smaller than** B (A ≥ B)
- `A $ B` → A is **not greater than** B (A ≤ B)
- `A % B` → A is **neither greater than nor equal to** B (A < B)
- `A & B` → A is **neither smaller than nor equal to** B (A > B)
- `A * B` → A is **neither greater than nor smaller than** B (A = B)

Statements and conclusions are given in code. Decide which conclusion(s) definitely follow.

**Q31.** Statements: P & Q, Q # R, R * S
Conclusions: I. P & S. II. R $ P.

**Q32.** Statements: A $ B, B % C, C # D
Conclusions: I. A % C. II. D $ B.

**Q33.** Statements: M # N, N * O, O $ P
Conclusions: I. M & P. II. P # M.

**Q34.** Statements: X & Y, Y & Z, Z # W
Conclusions: I. X & Z. II. X & W.

**Q35.** Statements: D # E, E $ F, F % G
Conclusions: I. D & G. II. D * E.

**Q36.** Statements: P % Q, Q * R, R & S
Conclusions: I. S % Q. II. P % R.

**Q37.** Statements: A * B, B $ C, C # D
Conclusions: I. A % C. II. A * C.

**Q38.** Statements: L & M, M # N, N % O
Conclusions: I. L & N. II. O & M.

**Q39.** Statements: R $ S, S % T, T * U
Conclusions: I. R % T. II. U & S.

**Q40.** Statements: P # Q, Q % R, R $ S
Conclusions: I. P & R. II. S & Q.

## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

### Master trick box

**The 6 forced (complementary) pairs.** An Either/Or verdict is possible ONLY when the two conclusions share the same subject and predicate and form one of these contradictory families — one member must be true in every scenario:

1. `All A are B`  ⇄  `Some A are not B`
2. `Some A are B`  ⇄  `No A is B`
3. `Some A are not B`  ⇄  `All A are B` (same family as 1, reversed listing)
4. `No A is B`  ⇄  `Some A are B` (same family as 2, reversed listing)
5. In coded/derived form: when you can prove `A ≤ C`, the pair `A < C` ⇄ `A = C` is forced.
6. In coded/derived form: when you can prove `A ≥ C`, the pair `A > C` ⇄ `A = C` is forced.

**Either/Or vs Neither test (2 gates):**
- Gate 1 — Individually neither conclusion follows on its own. (If one already follows, answer is a/b, never c.)
- Gate 2 — The two are a forced pair from the list above (contradictories / cover-all).
- Both gates pass → **Either (c)**. Gate 1 passes but Gate 2 fails (e.g. `Some A are B` + `Some A are not B`, which can both be false only if A is empty, i.e. they overlap and are NOT contradictories) → **Neither (d)**.

**Common trap:** `Some A are B` and `Some A are not B` look complementary but are NOT a forced pair — both can hold together, so failing both gives Neither, not Either.

### Syllogism solutions

**Q1 — e (Both).** Pens → inks → papers, so all pens are papers (I). "All pens are papers" gives the converse "some papers are pens" (II). **Trick:** a universal always yields its "some" converse.

**Q2 — e (Both).** The cats that are dogs are rats → some cats are rats (I); its converse gives some rats are cats (II).

**Q3 — e (Both).** Books are files and no file is a folder → no book is a folder (I). "All books are files" converts to "some files are books" (II).

**Q4 — a (Only I).** Apples that are red are sweet → some apples are sweet (I). "All apples are sweet" is not forced — non-red apples are unknown. **Trick:** a "some" statement never upgrades to "all".

**Q5 — d (Neither).** Roads are streets, but the lane-streets need not be roads → I and its converse II both fail. No definite link between the two "some/all + some" links through the shared middle.

**Q6 — c (Either).** Keys are locks; some locks are chains. The chain-locks may or may not be keys. Neither "some keys are chains" nor "no key is a chain" is forced (Gate 1), and they are the forced pair `Some A are B ⇄ No A is B` (Gate 2). **Trick:** a `Some/No` complementary pair, both undecided → Either.

**Q7 — c (Either).** Some tables are stools (via chairs) is certain, but "All tables are stools" (I) and "Some tables are not stools" (II) are each undecided and form the forced pair `All ⇄ Some-not`. **Trick:** the `All ⇄ Some-not` family is the second Either signature.

**Q8 — a (Only I).** Cats are pets and no cat is a dog → some pets (the cats) are not dogs (I). Dogs could still be pets, so II ("no dog is a pet") is not forced.

**Q9 — a (Only I).** "Only a few phones are smart" = some phones are smart **and** some are not. Smart phones are costly → some phones are costly (I). Non-smart phones' cost is unknown, so II is not forced. **Trick:** decode "only a few X are Y" as "some X are Y + some X are not Y".

**Q10 — a (Only I).** Some doctors are engineers (from "only a few") and no engineer is a lawyer → those doctors are not lawyers → I follows. Since those doctor-engineers can never be lawyers, "all doctors being lawyers" is impossible → II fails.

**Q11 — e (Both).** Cars → buses → trains, no train is a plane → no car is a plane (I). Buses are trains and no train is a plane → some buses are not planes (II).

**Q12 — e (Both).** Rose-flowers are not lilies → some flowers are not lilies (I). Lilies are plants and no rose is a lily → some plants (lilies) are not roses (II).

**Q13 — b (Only II).** Fruits are green, but the ripe-green need not be fruits → I ("some fruits are ripe") not forced. Ripe-green are not sour → some green are not sour (II).

**Q14 — c (Either).** Cups that are bottles are not boxes, but other cups may be boxes. "Some cups are boxes" (I) and "No cup is a box" (II) each undecided and form the `Some ⇄ No` forced pair → Either.

**Q15 — a (Only I).** Some dancers are actors, actors are stars → some dancers are stars (I). Singers are dancers but need not be the actor-dancers → II not forced.

**Q16 — a (Only I).** Wood is hard and no metal is wood → those hard things are not metal → some hard are not metal (I). II ("some metal are soft") has no chain support.

**Q17 — c (Either).** Chalks are dusters; some dusters are pens. The pen-dusters may or may not be chalks. "Some chalks are pens" (I) and "No chalk is a pen" (II) each undecided, forced `Some ⇄ No` pair → Either.

**Q18 — e (Both).** Teachers are wise, no wise is foolish → no teacher is foolish (I). Some foolish are clever and no wise is foolish → those clever are not wise → some clever are not wise (II).

**Q19 — e (Both).** Birds that are eagles are hawks → some birds are hawks (I). Eagles are hawks, no hawk is a crow → some eagles are not crows (II).

**Q20 — b (Only II).** Some mangoes are yellow (from "only a few"); no yellow is green → those mangoes are not green → some mangoes are not green (II). I ("some mangoes are green") is not forced.

### Inequality — Direct solutions

**Q21 — e (Both).** A > B ≥ C = D ⇒ A > D (I). B ≥ C = D > E ⇒ B > E, so B ≥ E holds (II).

**Q22 — e (Both).** P ≤ Q < R ⇒ P < R (I). Q < R = S ≤ T ⇒ Q < T, so Q ≤ T holds (II).

**Q23 — a (Only I).** L > M = N ≥ O ⇒ L > O (I). For II, both P and M sit above O (P > O, M ≥ O) with no direct link → P > M not forced.

**Q24 — c (Either).** A > B ≤ C = D breaks the chain at B (`> then ≤`), so A vs D is undecided. I (A > D) and II (A ≤ D) are the forced pair `> ⇄ ≤` → Either. **Trick:** a `>` immediately followed by `≤`/`<` (opposite-facing signs) snaps the chain; if conclusions are the complementary `>`/`≤` pair, answer Either.

**Q25 — c (Either).** P < Q = R ≥ S: the `< then ≥` break leaves P vs S undecided. I (P < S) and II (P ≥ S) are the forced `< ⇄ ≥` pair → Either.

**Q26 — e (Both).** M ≥ N > O = P ⇒ M > P (I). N > O = P ≥ Q ⇒ N > Q, so N ≥ Q holds (II).

**Q27 — e (Both).** D = E ≥ F ⇒ D ≥ F (I). E ≥ F > G ⇒ E > G (II).

**Q28 — b (Only II).** A < B = C > D: A and D are both below C with no link → I (A < D) not forced. B = C > D ≥ E ⇒ B > E (II).

**Q29 — d (Neither).** A ≤ B = C ⇒ A ≤ C, so "A < C" is not guaranteed (equality possible) → I fails. B = C ≥ D ≥ E ⇒ B ≥ E, so "B > E" not guaranteed → II fails. **Trick:** a strict conclusion (`<`,`>`) drawn from a chain containing `≥`/`≤`/`=` only is unsafe when equality can absorb the gap — that is a Neither, not an Either.

**Q30 — a (Only I).** L > M ≥ N ⇒ L > N (I). N = O ≤ P puts P above N; L is also above N, so L vs P undecided → II (L > P) fails.

### Inequality — Coded solutions

Decode first: `#`=≥, `$`=≤, `%`=<, `&`=>, `*`==.

**Q31 — e (Both).** P > Q, Q ≥ R, R = S. I: P > Q ≥ R = S ⇒ P > S = `P & S` ✓. II: P > Q ≥ R ⇒ P > R ⇒ R < P ⇒ R ≤ P = `R $ P` ✓.

**Q32 — a (Only I).** A ≤ B, B < C, C ≥ D. I: A ≤ B < C ⇒ A < C = `A % C` ✓. II: D ≤ C and B < C — D vs B undecided ⇒ `D $ B` not forced.

**Q33 — c (Either).** M ≥ N = O ≤ P. The `≥ … ≤` break leaves M vs P undecided. I: `M & P` (M > P). II: `P # M` (P ≥ M, i.e. M ≤ P). Forced `> ⇄ ≤` pair → Either.

**Q34 — e (Both).** X > Y, Y > Z, Z ≥ W. I: X > Y > Z ⇒ X > Z = `X & Z` ✓. II: X > Y > Z ≥ W ⇒ X > W = `X & W` ✓.

**Q35 — d (Neither).** D ≥ E, E ≤ F, F < G. I: D ≥ E ≤ F < G — D vs G undecided ⇒ `D & G` fails. II: D ≥ E allows D > E or D = E ⇒ `D * E` (D = E) not forced. Both fail → Neither.

**Q36 — e (Both).** P < Q, Q = R, R > S. I: S < R = Q ⇒ S < Q = `S % Q` ✓. II: P < Q = R ⇒ P < R = `P % R` ✓.

**Q37 — c (Either).** A = B ≤ C gives A ≤ C (proven). I: `A % C` (A < C). II: `A * C` (A = C). Since A ≤ C is established, exactly one of `<` / `=` holds → forced derived pair → Either. **Trick:** whenever you can prove `≤` (or `≥`) and the two conclusions are the matching `<`/`=` (or `>`/`=`) split, it is Either.

**Q38 — a (Only I).** L > M, M ≥ N, N < O. I: L > M ≥ N ⇒ L > N = `L & N` ✓. II: O > N and M ≥ N — O vs M undecided ⇒ `O & M` fails.

**Q39 — e (Both).** R ≤ S, S < T, T = U. I: R ≤ S < T ⇒ R < T = `R % T` ✓. II: U = T > S ⇒ U > S = `U & S` ✓.

**Q40 — b (Only II).** P ≥ Q, Q < R, R ≤ S. I: P ≥ Q < R — P vs R undecided ⇒ `P & R` fails. II: Q < R ≤ S ⇒ Q < S ⇒ S > Q = `S & Q` ✓.
