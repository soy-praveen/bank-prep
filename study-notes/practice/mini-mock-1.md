# Mixed Mini-Mock 1

> Timed: 22 min. Negative marking 0.25 — skip the sticky ones.

30 questions in real Prelims proportion: 12 English, 9 Quant, 9 Reasoning. Topics are jumbled the way an actual paper feels, so the real practice here is *switching gears* and *deciding fast what to attempt vs. leave*.

## Strategy

**Recommended attempt order: English -> Reasoning -> Quant.**

- **English first (Q sweep):** Do vocab, idiom, spelling, error-spotting and fills in one pass — these are 15-20 sec each and low-risk. Read the RC passage once, bank its 3 questions. Leave para jumble for a second pass if it doesn't click in 30 sec.
- **Reasoning second:** Grab syllogism, coding, blood relation, inequality, direction — all quick and near-certain. Attempt the puzzle set only if you can lock the arrangement in ~60 sec; otherwise move and come back.
- **Quant last:** Approximation, series, average, time-and-work, SI-type and the DI reads are the highest accuracy-per-second. Do the DI set together once you've read the table.
- **Skip discipline:** With 0.25 negative marking, a pure guess costs you. Only attempt when you've eliminated to 2 options or you actually know it. If short on time, the safe drops are the **puzzle set (Q13-15)**, the **para jumble (Q25)**, and the **DI ratio question (Q20)** — see the note at the end.

## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

**1. b) alleviate.** Mitigate = to make less severe. **Trick:** "mit-" softens (think *mild*); opposite of aggravate.

**2. c) Both I and II follow.** All pens -> books -> papers, so all pens are papers (I). "All pens are papers" converts to "some papers are pens" (II). **Trick:** A universal "All A are C" always yields the particular converse "Some C are A."

**3. c) 110.** 25% of 200 = 50; 15% of 400 = 60; 50 + 60 = 110. **Trick:** In approximation, round the ugly percentages (24.98 -> 25, 15.02 -> 15) before multiplying.

**4. b) was present.** With "Neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject "subordinates" (plural) -> "were present." **Trick:** Nor/or/either — verb matches the *nearest* noun.

**5. c) Sister.** Grandfather's only son = the man's father. Father's daughter = his sister. **Trick:** Collapse the chain to the nearest person: "only son of my grandfather" is almost always "my father."

**6. c) 60.** Differences are +1, +4, +9, +16 (perfect squares) -> next is +25 -> 35 + 25 = 60. **Trick:** When gaps grow fast, test 1,4,9,16 (squares) or cubes before anything else.

**7. b)** The passage's thesis is stated at the end: "not access alone but meaningful and sustained usage." **Trick:** For "primary purpose", find the sentence that *qualifies* or *pushes back* — usually near "yet/therefore".

**8. b)** Directly stated: genuine inclusion needs literacy, trust and products for low-income users; without them accounts sit idle. **Trick:** Detail questions are answered verbatim in the text — match, don't reason.

**9. b) inactive.** Dormant accounts = ones not being used. **Trick:** Read the vocab word back into its own sentence and swap in the option.

**10. a) MPBO.** BANK -> CBOL is each letter +1. LOAN: L+1=M, O+1=P, A+1=B, N+1=O -> MPBO. **Trick:** Compare position-by-position first; here every shift is exactly +1.

**11. d) x <= y.** I: (x-3)(x-4)=0 -> x = 3 or 4. II: (y-4)(y-5)=0 -> y = 4 or 5. Every x value <= every y value, so x <= y. **Trick:** List both root sets on a number line; if they only touch at one point, it's an "equal-or" (<= / >=) answer, not strict.

**12. a) compelling / reconsider.** "Despite strong evidence" sets up a contrast with refusing to rethink -> "compelling / reconsider" fits both slots logically. **Trick:** In double-fills, lock the blank you're surest of first (here "reconsider" after "refused to") and eliminate.

**13. c) R.** Given directly: R is on the topmost floor. **Trick:** Always cash the direct clue before deducing anything.

**14. e) T.** Arrangement: 1=S, 2=T, 3=P, 4=Q, 5=R. So floor 2 is T. **Trick:** P=3 and Q above it fixes 4; R=5; T-above-S must then be 1-2 in the only gap.

**15. c) 2.** Q is floor 4, S is floor 1; between them are floors 2 and 3 = 2 people (T and P). **Trick:** "Between" excludes the endpoints — count the floors strictly in the middle.

**16. c) 4% decrease.** 1.20 x 0.80 = 0.96 -> a 4% net fall. **Trick:** +a% then -a% always gives a fall of (a/10)^2 percent — here (20/10)^2 = 4%.

**17. a) most brilliant students.** "One of the + superlative" takes a plural noun. **Trick:** "One of the ..." is always followed by a plural.

**18. c) 5√2 m.** N5 -> (0,5); right = East 5 -> (5,5); right = South 10 -> (5,-5). Distance = √(5² + 5²) = √50 = 5√2. **Trick:** Sketch on axes; net displacement is the hypotenuse of the East and North-South legs.

**19. c) 660.** 120 + 150 + 180 + 210 = 660. **Trick:** Pair for speed: (120+180) + (150+210) = 300 + 360 = 660.

**20. c) 2022.** Ratios: 120/180 = 0.67, 150/200 = 0.75, 180/225 = 0.80, 210/300 = 0.70. Highest = 2022. **Trick:** You don't need exact decimals — 180/225 = 0.8 clearly beats 150/200 = 0.75; scan for the biggest numerator-vs-denominator gap.

**21. c) 50%.** (300 − 200)/200 x 100 = 50%. **Trick:** Increase from 200 to 300 is "half again" = 50%.

**22. c) unproductive.** Prolific = highly productive/fruitful; opposite is unproductive. **Trick:** "Pro-" + fertile sense; the antonym must carry a "barren/idle" meaning — only (c) does.

**23. c) Both I and II are true.** P > Q >= R = S, so P > S (I true). R = S < T, so T > R (II true). **Trick:** Follow the chain of signs; an equality (=) passes the inequality straight through.

**24. c) 38.** For consecutive evens the average is the middle term = 34 -> 30, 32, 34, 36, 38; largest = 38. **Trick:** Average of an odd count of evenly spaced numbers is the middle one; add 2+2 to reach the top.

**25. a) BCAD.** B introduces mobile banking, C gives the benefit, A the contrasting risk ("however"), D the bank's response ("therefore"). **Trick:** Find the opener (broad statement, no pronoun) then follow the connectors however/therefore to order the rest.

**26. b) 8 days.** 1/12 + 1/24 = 2/24 + 1/24 = 3/24 = 1/8 -> 8 days. **Trick:** LCM method: total work = 24 units, rates 2 + 1 = 3/day -> 24/3 = 8.

**27. b) to avoid coming to the main point.** Standard idiom for being evasive. **Trick:** "Around" the bush = never hitting the centre = dodging the point.

**28. e) 100.** 32, 64, 128, 256 are all powers of 2; 100 is not. **Trick:** Scan for a common rule (doubling here) and flag the one that breaks it.

**29. b) address.** To "address concerns" = to deal with them; the sentence is positive about the policy. **Trick:** The other four options are all negative (aggravate/ignore/dismiss/neglect) — the odd positive one is your answer.

**30. b) Occasion.** Double c, single s. **Trick:** Remember "oCCasion" — two c, one s, one n.
