| Finance | 300 | 50% |

**Q1.** What is the total number of male employees across all five departments?
a) 1090  b) 1150  c) 1190  d) 1210  e) 1290

**Q2.** The ratio of total male employees to total female employees in the firm is:
a) 81:119  b) 119:81  c) 7:5  d) 5:7  e) 3:2

**Q3.** The number of female employees in IT and Sales together is:

**Q4.** What is the difference between the number of male employees in Marketing and the number of female employees in HR?

## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

### Set 1

**Q1 — (c) 1190.** Males = Total − Females per row.
IT: 600 − 40% = 360; HR: 250 − 60% = 100; Sales: 450 − 20% = 360; Marketing: 400 − 45% = 220; Finance: 300 − 50% = 150. Sum = 360 + 100 + 360 + 220 + 150 = **1190**.
**Trick:** Male% = (100 − Female%). Compute males directly as Total × Male% in one pass instead of finding females first: 600×60% + 250×40% + 450×80% + 400×55% + 300×50%.

**Q2 — (b) 119:81.** Total employees = 600+250+450+400+300 = 2000. Females = 2000 − 1190 = 810. Ratio = 1190:810 = **119:81** (divide both by 10).
**Trick:** Once you have one part and the grand total, the other part is just a subtraction — no need to re-add all the female figures.

**Q3 — (c) 330.** Females: IT = 240, Sales = 90. 240 + 90 = **330**.
**Trick:** 40% of 600 = 240 and 20% of 450 = 90 are one-step mental products (move the decimal, take the fraction).

**Q4 — (c) 70.** Males in Marketing = 220; Females in HR = 150. 220 − 150 = **70**.
**Trick:** 45% of 400 = 180 female, so male = 220. HR female 60% of 250 = 150. Keep a running male/female table as you read — it serves every sub-question.

**Q5 — (d) 12.5%.** (450 − 400)/400 × 100 = 50/400 = **12.5%**.
**Trick:** "One-eighth = 12.5%." Since the extra 50 is 1/8 of 400, recognise the fraction instead of dividing.

### Set 2

**Q6 — (c) 70 kmph.** Speed = 420 ÷ 6 = **70 kmph**.

**Q7 — (c) 62 kmph.** Speeds: A = 300/5 = 60, B = 70, C = 400/5 = 80, D = 360/8 = 45, E = 550/10 = 55. Average = (60+70+80+45+55)/5 = 310/5 = **62**.
**Trick:** Average of speeds ≠ total distance ÷ total time here (times differ). Since the question says "average speed of the trains," average the five individual speeds.

**Q8 — (c) 2030 km.** 300 + 420 + 400 + 360 + 550 = **2030**.
**Trick:** Pair to round numbers: (300+400) + (420+... ) → 700 + 550 = 1250, +420 = 1670, +360 = 2030.

**Q9 — (b) 3:4.** Speed A : Speed C = 60 : 80 = **3:4**.

**Q10 — (c) 6 hours.** New speed = 45 + 15 = 60 kmph. Time = 360 ÷ 60 = **6 hours**.
**Trick:** Distance is fixed, so time is inversely proportional to speed: old time 8 h at 45 → 45×8 = 360; divide by the new 60 to get 6.

### Set 3

**Q11 — (c) 1500.** 200 + 250 + 300 + 350 + 400 = **1500**.
**Trick:** The X values form an arithmetic series (step 50). Sum = average × count = 300 × 5 = 1500.

**Q12 — (d) 2022.** Combined totals: 2019 = 350, 2020 = 550, 2021 = 550, 2022 = **750**, 2023 = 720. Highest is 2022.
**Trick:** Only the top few candidates matter — eyeball the two tallest pairs (2022 and 2023) and add just those.

**Q13 — (c) 80.** X total = 1500; Y total = 150+300+250+400+320 = 1420. Difference = 1500 − 1420 = **80**.

**Q14 — (c) 120%.** 300 ÷ 250 × 100 = **120%**.
**Trick:** 300/250 = 6/5 = 1.2 → 120%.

**Q15 — (c) 300.** 1500 ÷ 5 = **300** (already computed in Q11).

### Set 4

**Q16 — (c) 15,000.** 25% of 60,000 = **15,000**.
**Trick:** 25% = one-quarter; just divide 60,000 by 4.

**Q17 — (c) 3,000.** Education 15% = 9,000; Transport 10% = 6,000. Difference = the 5% gap = 5% of 60,000 = **3,000**.
**Trick:** Skip both amounts — take the percentage difference (15% − 10% = 5%) of the total directly.

**Q18 — (c) 72.** 20% of 360° = **72°**.
**Trick:** 1% = 3.6°, so 20% = 20 × 3.6 = 72.

**Q19 — (c) 27,000.** (25% + 20%) = 45% of 60,000 = **27,000**.

**Q20 — (c) 15,000.** 20% of 75,000 = **15,000**.
**Trick:** Savings is 1/5 of income; 75,000 ÷ 5 = 15,000.

### Set 5

**Q21 — (c) 345 thousand.** 40+55+50+68+72+60 = **345**.
**Trick:** Group: (40+60) + (55+72) + (50+68) = 100 + 127 + 118 = 345.

**Q22 — (c) 80%.** (72 − 40)/40 × 100 = 32/40 × 100 = **80%**.
**Trick:** 32/40 = 4/5 = 80%.

**Q23 — (c) Apr.** Month-on-month changes: Feb +15, Mar −5, Apr +18, May +4, Jun −12. Largest rise = **Apr (+18)**.
**Trick:** Only compare the "up" jumps; Feb (+15) and Apr (+18) are the contenders — Apr wins.

**Q24 — (c) 57.5 thousand.** 345 ÷ 6 = **57.5**.
**Trick:** Reuse the Q21 total; 345/6 = 57.5.

**Q25 — (a) 11:12.** 55 : 60 = **11:12** (divide both by 5).

### Set 6

**Q26 — (b) 2,000.** Males = 8,000 × 5/8 = 5,000. Literate males = 40% of 5,000 = **2,000**.
**Trick:** Ratio 5:3 over 8 parts means 1 part = 8,000/8 = 1,000; males = 5 parts = 5,000.

**Q27 — (c) 3,800.** Females = 3 parts = 3,000. Literate females = 60% of 3,000 = 1,800. Total literate = 2,000 + 1,800 = **3,800**.

**Q28 — (c) 1,200.** Illiterate females = 40% of 3,000 = **1,200** (or 3,000 − 1,800).

**Q29 — (d) 66.67%.** (5,000 − 3,000)/3,000 × 100 = 2,000/3,000 = **66.67%**.
**Trick:** With ratio 5:3, "how much more is 5 than 3" = 2/3 = 66.67% — read it straight off the ratio, no headcounts needed.

**Q30 — (b) 10:9.** Literate males : literate females = 2,000 : 1,800 = **10:9** (divide both by 200).
