### Reading Comprehension — Passage 1

India's push toward a less-cash economy has produced a curious paradox. Even as digital payments through unified interfaces have grown to record volumes, the amount of physical currency in circulation has also risen steadily. Economists offer two explanations. First, digital transactions have largely replaced cheques and card swipes rather than cash itself; people still hold notes as a store of value and for informal, small-ticket trade. Second, cash remains a hedge against uncertainty. During periods of economic stress, households instinctively withdraw and hoard currency, trusting a note in hand over a balance on a screen.

The lesson for policymakers is that digital adoption and cash usage are not strictly a zero-sum game. Building payment rails is necessary but not sufficient. Unless citizens feel that the banking system is secure, accessible, and forgiving of error, the reassuring weight of paper money will not disappear. Financial inclusion, in this reading, is less a matter of technology than of trust.

### Reading Comprehension — Passage 2

When a large public-sector bank recently announced that it would keep several rural branches open despite their thin profits, market analysts were quick to call the decision uncommercial. Yet the bank's chairperson defended it in plainly strategic terms. A branch, she argued, is not merely a counter for deposits; it is the visible face of the institution in places where a mobile signal is unreliable and where an elderly farmer may never trust an app. To withdraw is to surrender the field to informal moneylenders whose interest rates trap the very borrowers a public bank is meant to protect.

Her argument reframed the debate. Profitability measured branch by branch tells only part of the story. A rural presence generates goodwill, gathers low-cost deposits, and builds the credit histories that make future lending safer. Seen over a decade rather than a quarter, the "loss-making" branch may be among the bank's soundest long-term investments. The real question, she suggested, is not whether the branch pays, but over what horizon we choose to measure.

## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

**Q1 — (b).** The passage's whole arc is that digital growth has not killed cash, and the closing line names trust, not technology, as the driver. (a), (c), (d), (e) each contradict or overstate the text.
**Trick (RC main idea):** the correct answer must cover the *whole* passage, not one supporting detail. Reject options that are true but narrow, and reject any that go beyond what the text says.

**Q2 — (b).** Stated directly: digital transactions "largely replaced cheques and card swipes rather than cash," and people "still hold notes as a store of value."
**Trick (detail):** locate the exact line; the right option paraphrases it without adding new claims.

**Q3 — (a).** The final sentences tie cash-holding to a lack of trust in banks; so building secure, accessible, forgiving banking would logically lower cash dependence. (c) contradicts "not strictly a zero-sum game."
**Trick (inference):** a valid inference is one small logical step from the text, never a leap. Extreme words ("vanish within a year") usually signal a wrong option.

**Q4 — (b).** "Cash remains a hedge against uncertainty" — protection against possible loss.
**Trick (vocab-in-context):** judge the word only from *its sentence*, not its most common dictionary meaning. "Hedge" as shrubs (a) is common but wrong here.

**Q5 — (b).** The writer weighs two explanations and draws a measured lesson — no mockery, alarm, or celebration.
**Trick (tone):** match the author's stance to the language. Balanced, explanatory prose is "analytical," never "triumphant" or "fearful" unless emotive words appear.

**Q6 — (b).** The closing question — "over what horizon we choose to measure" — is the thesis: long-run value versus quarterly profit.
**Trick (RC main idea):** the concluding sentence often states the main point; check that your choice reflects it and spans the passage.

**Q7 — (b).** "To withdraw is to surrender the field to informal moneylenders whose interest rates trap the very borrowers a public bank is meant to protect."
**Trick (detail):** answer must restate the cause-effect the author actually gives, not a tempting opposite.

**Q8 — (a).** The chairperson argues profitability "measured branch by branch tells only part of the story," implying short-term branch accounting understates real value.
**Trick (inference):** pick the option the author would *agree* with; avoid absolutes like "guaranteed" and "every."

**Q9 — (a).** "Surrender the field to informal moneylenders" = give up control to them.
**Trick (vocab-in-context):** substitute your candidate word back into the sentence; only "give up control" keeps the meaning.

**Q10 — (b).** She actively defends the branches "in plainly strategic terms" as sound long-term investments — reasoned, not apologetic.
**Trick (tone/attitude):** read the verbs describing the speaker ("defended," "argued"); a defender is not "indifferent" or "hostile."

**Q11 — (b).** "Sign up for credit *without* understanding its true cost." Meaning: lacking that understanding.
**Trick (cloze):** read the whole passage first for sense; here the logic needs "absence of understanding," so "without" fits, not the contrast word "despite."

**Q12 — (c).** "*Unless* compound interest is explained early, borrowers tend to underestimate..." — the shortfall happens in the absence of teaching, a conditional-negative link.
**Trick (cloze connectors):** connectors set polarity. The clause describes a bad outcome when teaching is missing, so "Unless" (= if not) beats "Because" or "Although."

**Q13 — (b).** Good programmes "*reduce* default rates" — positive effect, so the debt-failure measure should fall.
**Trick (cloze):** align the blank's polarity with the sentence's positive tone; teaching budgeting lowers (not raises) defaults.

**Q14 — (c).** A wise population "places *less* strain on welfare systems" — fewer problems, so lower strain.
**Trick (cloze):** trace the cause (wise money management) to a favourable effect (reduced burden) — "less," not "more/greater."

**Q15 — (a).** Lessons rarely "*translate* into real behaviour" — the fixed collocation is "translate into."
**Trick (cloze collocation):** trust the idiom; "translate into action/behaviour" is standard, unlike "transform/transfer into."

**Q16 — (c).** Short modules can "*build* lasting habits" — the positive counter to the critics, reinforced by "lasting."
**Trick (cloze):** the sentence rebuts the critics, so the verb must be constructive; "build habits" pairs naturally with "lasting."

**Q17 — (b).** Facing "stricter audits" is a penalty, so the trigger must be negative conduct: lenders who *ignore* complaints.
**Trick (fillers):** the consequence signals the polarity — a punishment follows bad behaviour, so pick the negative verb.

**Q18 — (a).** "Although... great fanfare, benefits reached *surprisingly* few" — the contrast word "although" demands a let-down, and "surprisingly few" captures it.
**Trick (fillers):** the connector "although" forces a reversal; only a word conveying shortfall fits.

**Q19 — (b).** "*both* ... *and*" — the report satisfied critics and guided reform; two positives joined.
**Trick (double fillers):** test paired correlatives as a unit; "both...and" links two co-existing qualities, while "neither...nor" would wrongly deny both.

**Q20 — (b).** "Rising costs *coupled with* weak demand have *squeezed* profits" — two pressures combine to cut profit.
**Trick (double fillers):** both blanks must share one direction; two negatives (higher costs + weak demand) logically "squeeze," not "boost," profits.

**Q21 — (b).** "Preferring gradual reform to sudden change" defines a *cautious* approach.
**Trick (fillers):** let the descriptive clause define the blank; "gradual, not disruptive" equals cautious.

**Q22 — (b).** "*While* the audit revealed no fraud, it did expose several *weaknesses*" — contrast connector plus a flaw in "internal controls."
**Trick (double fillers):** "did expose" signals a contrast to "no fraud," so use "While"; controls are exposed for weaknesses, not strengths.
