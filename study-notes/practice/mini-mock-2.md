# Mixed Mini-Mock 2

Timed: 22 min. Negative marking 0.25.

SBI PO Prelims level. 30 questions in real exam proportion — 12 English, 9 Quant, 9 Reasoning — topics jumbled like a real paper. Contains one DI caselet set (Q14-16) and one linear-seating set (Q8-10).

## Strategy

- **Attempt order:** First sweep the standalone one-liners you can crack in under 40s — simplification, synonym/idiom, direction, ranking, blood relation, coding. Second pass: fillers, error-spotting, quadratic comparison, series. Save the two *sets* (seating Q8-10, DI caselet Q14-16) and the para jumble (Q18) for last — they are high-reward but time-hungry.
- **Target:** ~20 attempts at 90%+ accuracy beats 30 attempts at 70% once you subtract 0.25 per wrong answer.
- **Skip guidance:** If a set won't "click" in 60s, leave the whole block and move on — never burn 4 minutes hunting one seating arrangement. Skip-if-stuck items are flagged in the answer key.
- **Negative marking math:** 4 wrong guesses wipe out 1 correct answer. Only guess when you have eliminated 2+ options.

## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

**1. (a) A.** "Committee" here acts as a single unit, so it takes a singular verb: "has decided," not "have decided." **Trick:** Collective nouns (committee, jury, team, government) take a singular verb when the group acts as one body; only use a plural verb when members act individually.

**2. (c) Both I and II follow.** All pens → pencils → erasers, so "All pens are erasers" (I) is valid. Since all pens are erasers, "Some erasers are pens" (II) is the valid converse. **Trick:** A-type + A-type in a chain gives an A-type conclusion; any universal ("all X are Y") automatically yields the particular converse "some Y are X."

**3. (b) 153.** 45% of 240 = 108; 30% of 150 = 45; 108 + 45 = 153. **Trick:** 45% of 240 = 240 × 0.45 = 108 fast if you read it as "half of 240 minus 5% of 240" = 120 − 12 = 108.

**4. (a) damning / composed.** "Despite" signals contrast: strong (damning) evidence, yet the accused stayed calm (composed). Options (c) and (e) pair a strong first word with a *matching* reaction, breaking the required contrast. **Trick:** With "Despite/In spite of," the two blanks must pull in opposite directions.

**5. (c) 60.** Differences are perfect squares: +1, +4, +9, +16, so next is +25 → 35 + 25 = 60. **Trick:** When gaps grow fast, test the difference pattern for 1², 2², 3², 4²...

**6. (a) P > T.** Chain: P ≥ Q > R = S ≥ T. The strict ">" between Q and R forces Q > T, and P ≥ Q, so P > T. **Trick:** A relation is strict end-to-end only if at least one ">" (or "<") lies on the path between the two variables; "≥" links alone would give "≥."

**7. (b) to avoid the main topic.** "Beat around the bush" = to speak evasively instead of coming to the point. **Trick:** Memorise it as the opposite of "get straight to the point."

**Seating solution (Q8-10):** R is 3rd from left → position 3. Q immediately right of R → 4. T immediately left of P and P at an extreme end: P can't be position 1 (no seat to its left), so P = 7, T = 6. "Two people between Q(4) and S" with S = 7 clashes with P, so S = 1 (positions 2,3 lie between). V immediately right of S → V = 2. That leaves U at 5 (non-extreme, satisfied). Final row: **S, V, R, Q, U, T, P** (positions 1-7).

**8. (b) S.** Extreme left = position 1 = S. **Trick:** Anchor the fixed position (R at 3) first, then the "immediately right/left" clues cascade.

**9. (b) 2.** R = 3, T = 6; between them are positions 4 and 5 (Q and U) = 2 people. **Trick:** "Between" excludes the endpoints — count only the seats strictly in between.

**10. (b) Q.** U = 5; immediately to its left is position 4 = Q. **Trick:** Once the full line is fixed, "immediately left" = position number minus 1.

**11. (d) x ≤ y.** x² − 7x + 12 = 0 → x = 3, 4. y² − 9y + 20 = 0 → y = 4, 5. Largest x (4) is not greater than smallest y (4), so x ≤ y in every pairing. **Trick:** Compare the max of one variable's roots against the min of the other's — if max(x) ≤ min(y), the answer is x ≤ y.

**12. (b) when.** "Scarcely/Hardly ... when" is the fixed correlative pair. **Trick:** Scarcely/Hardly → "when"; No sooner → "than." Don't mix them.

**13. (b) wise.** Prudent = careful, wise, showing good judgment. Antonym is reckless (a). **Trick:** Link prudent ↔ prudence ↔ "prude-nt planning" = cautious foresight.

**DI caselet solution (Q14-16):** Total = 300. Pens = 40% × 300 = 120. Remaining (notebooks + erasers) = 180. Notebooks = erasers + 90, so 2 × erasers + 90 = 180 → erasers = 45, notebooks = 135.

**14. (b) 135.** As solved above. **Trick:** Reduce two-item "sum and difference" problems to erasers = (180 − 90)/2 = 45 instantly.

**15. (a) 8 : 3.** Pens : erasers = 120 : 45 = 8 : 3. **Trick:** Divide both by the HCF (15) in one step.

**16. (c) 200%.** (135 − 45)/45 × 100 = 90/45 × 100 = 200%. **Trick:** When the bigger value is 3× the smaller, it is exactly 200% more (not 300%).

**17. (a) BQQMF.** MANGO → NBOHP is a +1 shift on each letter. APPLE → B, Q, Q, M, F = BQQMF. **Trick:** Verify the rule on the given pair first (M→N confirms +1), then apply blindly.

**18. (a) BCAD.** B introduces the topic (smartphones indispensable); C elaborates what they store; A ("However ... hidden cost to privacy") pivots; D ("therefore ... vigilant") concludes. **Trick:** Locate the opener (a general statement with no back-reference) and let signal words "However" and "therefore" fix the middle and end.

**19. (c) 15.** Son = s, father = 3s. After 5 yrs: 3s + 5 = 2.5(s + 5) → 3s + 5 = 2.5s + 12.5 → 0.5s = 7.5 → s = 15. Check: 45 & 15 → after 5, 50 & 20 = 2.5×. **Trick:** Clear the decimal by doubling: 6s + 10 = 5s + 25 → s = 15.

**20. (b) B.** "Each of the students" takes a singular verb → "was given," not "were given." **Trick:** "Each / Every / Either / Neither / One of + plural noun" always takes a singular verb.

**21. (b) Sister.** Grandfather's only son = the man's father; the father's daughter = the man's sister. **Trick:** Solve the innermost relation first ("only son" = father), then move outward.

**Cloze solution (Q22-24):** The passage argues financial literacy is essential in a digital economy.

**22. (b) necessity.** "Not a luxury but a ______" needs the opposite of luxury → necessity. **Trick:** "not X but Y" demands Y to contrast X.

**23. (a) dominate.** Digital transactions ______ everyday life → dominate (fill/pervade). **Trick:** Pick the verb that keeps the sentence's positive-to-warning momentum; "ignore/escape/abandon" contradict "in a world where."

**24. (b) shield.** "Shield ordinary people from costly mistakes" is the idiomatic collocation. "Prevent" would need "from making" to be correct. **Trick:** "shield/protect/save someone FROM something" — match the preposition to the verb.

**25. (c) ₹920.** 15% of 800 = 120; 800 + 120 = 920. **Trick:** 15% = 10% + 5% = 80 + 40 = 120.

**26. (a) methodical.** The approach "impressed the panel," so a positive word is needed → methodical (orderly, systematic). **Trick:** Match blank polarity to the outcome word; "impressed" demands a positive filler.

**27. (a) 3 km.** North 5 → right (east) 3 → right (south) 5. The 5 north and 5 south cancel; net displacement = 3 km east. **Trick:** Sketch it; equal opposite legs cancel, leaving only the perpendicular leg as the answer.

**28. (c) 72.** Speed = 180/9 = 20 m/s = 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h. **Trick:** Convert m/s → km/h by ×18/5 (and km/h → m/s by ×5/18).

**29. (b) of.** "Accused of" is the fixed preposition. **Trick:** accused OF a crime, but charged WITH a crime — don't swap them.

**30. (b) 29.** Total = rank from top + rank from bottom − 1 = 12 + 18 − 1 = 29. **Trick:** Always subtract 1 because the person is counted twice.
