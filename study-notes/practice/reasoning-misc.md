# Blood Relations, Direction, Coding, Alphanumeric & Ranking — Practice

Moderate SBI PO Prelims set: 20 questions across five miscellaneous reasoning topics, each with a worked solution and a reusable trick.

## Questions

### Blood Relations

1. In a coded relation system: `P + Q` means P is the father of Q, `P - Q` means P is the sister of Q, `P × Q` means P is the brother of Q, and `P ÷ Q` means P is the mother of Q. How is A related to D in the expression **A + B × C ÷ D**?
   - (a) Father
   - (b) Maternal grandfather
   - (c) Paternal grandfather
   - (d) Maternal uncle
   - (e) Cannot be determined

2. Pointing to a photograph, a man said, "I have no brother or sister, but that man's father is my father's son." Who is the man in the photograph?
   - (a) His father
   - (b) His son
   - (c) His cousin
   - (d) His uncle
   - (e) Himself

3. A man, pointing towards a lady, says, "Her mother is the only daughter of my mother." How is the man related to the lady?
   - (a) Father
   - (b) Brother
   - (c) Maternal uncle
   - (d) Grandfather
   - (e) Son

4. A and B are siblings. C is the daughter of A. D is the brother of B. E is the mother of D. How is E related to C?
   - (a) Mother
   - (b) Aunt
   - (c) Grandmother
   - (d) Sister
   - (e) Cannot be determined

5. In a family, `M @ N` means M is the wife of N, `M # N` means M is the son of N, and `M & N` means M is the daughter of N. In **P @ Q # R & S**, how is P related to S?
   - (a) Daughter-in-law
   - (b) Granddaughter
   - (c) Granddaughter-in-law (wife of grandson)
   - (d) Daughter
   - (e) Cannot be determined

## Answer Key, Solutions & Tricks

**1. (b) Maternal grandfather.**
`A + B` -> A is father of B. `B × C` -> B is brother of C, so A is also father of C. `C ÷ D` -> C is mother of D, so C is female. A is the father of C (D's mother), i.e. A is D's mother's father = maternal grandfather of D.
> **Trick:** Solve coded relations symbol by symbol, left to right, and pin down each person's gender as you go (here C is female because C is a "mother"). The parent of the mother is always the *maternal* grandparent.

**2. (b) His son.**
"My father's son" — since the speaker has no brother, that son is the speaker himself. So "that man's father" is the speaker. Therefore the man in the photograph is the speaker's son.
> **Trick — self-reference:** Whenever you see "my father's only son / my mother's only son" and the speaker is male with no brothers, it collapses to *the speaker himself*. Substitute that in and re-read the sentence.

**3. (c) Maternal uncle.**
"The only daughter of my mother" — the man cannot be a daughter, so this is his sister. That sister is the lady's mother. So the man is the brother of the lady's mother = her maternal uncle.
> **Trick:** For "only son/only daughter of my X" phrases, first identify who that unique person is from the speaker's side of the generation tree, then trace forward to the target person.

**4. (c) Grandmother.**
A and B are siblings; D is the brother of B, so D is also a sibling of A. E is the mother of D, hence E is the mother of A, B and D. C is the daughter of A, so E (A's mother) is C's grandmother.
> **Trick — generation tree:** Draw horizontal lines for siblings (A, B, D on one level) and vertical lines for parent-child. Once everyone sits on the correct generation line, the relation is read off directly: two levels up = grand-relation.

**5. (c) Granddaughter-in-law (wife of grandson).**
`R & S` -> R is daughter of S (R is female). `Q # R` -> Q is son of R, so Q is the grandson of S. `P @ Q` -> P is wife of Q. Therefore P is the wife of S's grandson = granddaughter-in-law of S.
> **Trick:** Resolve the chain from the far end (S) inward, marking gender from each defining word ("daughter" -> female, "son" -> male, "wife" -> female). Q is two generations below S, so P (Q's wife) is a "grand-in-law".

**6. (c) 10 km.**
North and East are perpendicular, so the displacement is the hypotenuse: distance = √(6² + 8²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 km.
> **Trick — Pythagoras:** When the two legs of the walk are at right angles, shortest distance = √(a² + b²). Memorise the 3-4-5, 6-8-10 and 5-12-13 triples to spot the answer instantly.

**7. (a) 13 km, North-East.**
North 12 km, then a right turn (now facing East) 5 km East. Displacement = √(12² + 5²) = √(144 + 25) = √169 = 13 km. Net movement is North and East, so the final position is to the North-East of home.
> **Trick — "with respect to":** The reference point (home) is the origin. Net Northward + net Eastward movement lands you in the North-East quadrant relative to it. Use the 5-12-13 triple for the distance.

**8. (a) 5 m North.**
Set A = (0, 0). B = (5, 0) [East]. C = (5, 5) [North of B]. D = (0, 5) [West of C]. D is directly above A, so D is 5 m North of A.
> **Trick:** Convert each step to coordinates (East = +x, North = +y). "D with respect to A" means treat A as origin and read D's coordinates: same x, higher y -> due North.

**9. (c) North.**
Start North. 90° clockwise -> East. 180° anticlockwise from East -> West. 90° clockwise from West -> North.
> **Trick:** Clockwise adds degrees, anticlockwise subtracts, on the compass N(0)-E(90)-S(180)-W(270). Track only the net turn: +90 − 180 + 90 = 0, so he ends up facing the original North.

**10. (a) EPH.**
Each letter shifts +1: C->D, A->B, T->U (given). So D->E, O->P, G->H = EPH.
> **Trick — find the shift from one letter:** Compare just one letter of the code with its original (C->D = +1). Apply that same shift to every letter of the new word.

**11. (a) FZQCDM.**
FLOWER -> EKNVDQ is a −1 shift (F->E, L->K, O->N, W->V, E->D, R->Q). Apply −1 to GARDEN: G->F, A->Z, R->Q, D->C, E->D, N->M = FZQCDM. (A wraps back to Z.)
> **Trick:** For backward shifts, remember A wraps to Z. Verify the shift on two letters before applying it to the whole word.

**12. (d) ta.**
"is" appears in all three sentences and `pa` appears in all three -> is = pa. "she" is common to sentences 1 and 3 (`la`) -> she = la. "smart" is common to sentences 2 and 3 (`sa`) -> smart = sa. In sentence 1, the leftover word "intelligent" maps to the leftover code `ta`.
> **Trick — match the common word:** Line up sentences that share a word; the code they share is that word. Eliminate the known words in a sentence, and whatever word and code are left must pair up.

**13. (a) 2-5-1-4.**
Positional (A=1 … Z=26) coding: B=2, E=5, A=1, D=4 -> 2-5-1-4.
> **Trick — position value:** Learn anchor positions (A=1, E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Z=26) and count from the nearest anchor to get any letter's number fast.

**14. (b) WLT.**
Opposite-letter coding (A<->Z, positions add to 27): C(3)->X(24), A(1)->Z(26), T(20)->G(7) — all pairs sum to 27. For DOG: D(4)->W(23), O(15)->L(12), G(7)->T(20). Check: 4+23 = 15+12 = 7+20 = 27. Answer = WLT.
> **Trick — opposite letters sum to 27:** The mirror of a letter at position n sits at position (27 − n). A<->Z, B<->Y, C<->X, M<->N. Compute 27 − n and convert back to a letter.

**15. (b) Two.**
Series: `7 B % 3 P @ K 9 R # 2 M W 5 & T 8 * N 4 Q`. Symbols and their neighbours — `%` (before B/letter: no), `@` (before P/letter: no), `#` (before R/letter: no), `&` (before 5/number, after T/letter: **yes**), `*` (before 8/number, after N/letter: **yes**). Count = 2.
> **Trick — number-symbol-letter:** Scan only the symbols, then check the "preceded-by / followed-by" condition one symbol at a time. Read the pattern as [number][symbol][letter].

**16. (c) Five.**
Numbers followed by a letter: 7->B (yes), 3->P (yes), 9->R (yes), 2->M (yes), 5->& (no), 8->* (no), 4->Q (yes). Total = 5.
> **Trick:** For "immediately followed by" questions, look only at the element to the *right* of each target; ignore everything else in the series.

**17. (a) M.**
The 15th element from the left is `&`. Third to its left is 15 − 3 = 12th element = `M`.
> **Trick:** "kth to the left of the mth from the left" = the (m − k)th element from the left. Here 15 − 3 = 12, so just count to the 12th element.

**18. (c) 17.**
Total = (rank from left) + (rank from right) − 1 = 7 + 11 − 1 = 17.
> **Trick — total formula:** When both ranks point to the *same* person, Total = left rank + right rank − 1 (subtract 1 so the person isn't counted twice).

**19. (b) 29th.**
Rank from bottom = Total − rank from top + 1 = 40 − 12 + 1 = 29.
> **Trick:** Rank from bottom = Total − rank from top + 1. It's the "total − 1" formula rearranged for one known rank.

**20. (c) 6.**
A is 10th from the left. B is 9th from the right in a row of 25, so B's position from the left = 25 − 9 + 1 = 17. Children between them = 17 − 10 − 1 = 6.
> **Trick:** Convert both people to positions from the *same* end, then children between = (higher position − lower position − 1). Subtracting the extra 1 excludes the two endpoints themselves.
