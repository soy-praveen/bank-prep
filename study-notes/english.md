## Error-Spotting Rulebook (complete)

### 1. Subject–Verb Agreement
- "The number of" + plural → **singular** verb. ("A number of" → plural.)
- "One of the" + superlative + **plural** noun.
- **Ignore "of ___" phrases** between subject and verb. *The quality of the products **is** good* (subject = quality).
- "Along with / as well as / together with / including" **don't change** the subject.
- **Neither-nor / Either-or → verb agrees with the NEARER subject.**
- Each / Every / Everyone → **singular**.
- Uncountables (information, furniture, advice, news, luggage, equipment) → singular, no "a", no plural.

### 2. Tenses
- **Sequence of tenses:** past main clause → past subordinate (*He said he **would** come*).
- **Past Perfect** for the earlier of two past actions (*The train **had left** before I reached*).
- Universal truths stay present.
- **No "will" in time/if clauses** (*When he **comes**…* / *If it **rains**…*).
- Present perfect with **since/for** (*I **have lived** here for 5 years*).

### 3. Conditionals
- Type 1: If + present, **will** + verb. Type 2: If + past, **would** + verb.
- Type 3: If + **had** + V3, **would have** + V3. **Never "if + would."**

### 4. Articles (sound, not letter)
*an hour, a university, an MBA, an honest man.* "the" for unique/specific.

### 5. Pronouns
- Case: *Between you and **me*** (object). Subject (I/he) vs object (me/him).
- Agreement with antecedent. Reflexive can't be a subject.

### 6. Conjunctions / Correlatives (pairs must match)
not only…**but also** · either…**or** · neither…**nor** · both…**and** · **no sooner…than** (not "then") · hardly/scarcely…**when** · so/such…**that**

### 7. Adjectives vs Adverbs
- Adverb modifies verbs (*drives **carefully***).
- **Two → comparative** (taller); **3+ → superlative** (tallest).
- No double comparison; *unique/perfect/complete* can't be compared.

### 8. Countable vs Uncountable
- fewer/many/number → countable · less/much/amount → uncountable.
- *Fewer books, less water. Much work* (not "many works").

### 9. Prepositions (fixed pairs)
senior/superior/junior/inferior/prefer → **to** · different **from** · good **at** · married **to** · capable **of**

### 10. Redundancy (remove the extra word)
~~return **back**~~ · ~~repeat **again**~~ · ~~cousin **brother**~~ · ~~more **superior**~~ · ~~reason is **because**~~

## Reading Comprehension (your strength — 8–10 marks)
- Main-idea questions: pick the option that covers the WHOLE passage, not one detail.
- Vocab-in-context: read the sentence, judge meaning from context.

## Fillers
Read the **connector logic** — "despite/although" = contrast; "because/therefore" = cause. Pick words that fit the logic, not just the meaning.

## Para-Jumbles (logic puzzles — a strength once you know the moves)
1. **Find the OPENER:** stands alone, introduces the topic with a **full noun**, no back-references (not *however/this/it/therefore*).
2. **Find the CLOSER:** concludes — *thus/finally/as a result/today*.
3. **Link the middle:**
   - **Pronouns hug their noun** — "It/This/These/He" comes right after the sentence naming the noun.
   - Connectors: *However/But* (contrast) · *Therefore/Thus* (result) · *Also/Moreover* (addition) · *For example* (follows a claim).
   - **a → the:** "a scientist" introduced first, "the scientist" refers back.
   - **Mandatory pairs:** two sentences glued by a reference — find these first, they lock big chunks.
- Tiebreak between two consequence sentences: the one whose pronoun points at the previous line comes first; the "as a result/finally" one closes.

## Cloze Test (RC + grammar + vocab combined)
1. **Read the whole passage first** for the theme — don't fill blindly.
2. Each blank: context logic + correct **part of speech** + **collocation** (words that go together).
3. **Connectors decide polarity:** *despite/although/yet* → the blank contrasts; *because/therefore* → it matches.
